# Art checklist — every piece must pass every item

This list was built from the author's feedback on his first game, *Heisty Spideys*:
a pencil half on a notepad with its other end hovering over the table, floating
food, a cat with one eye, a towel with no rack, a half-drawn hook cut off by the
frame, a bottle cap at an impossible angle, a launcher that couldn't work, and
scenes too sparse on detail. Every one of those classes of mistake applies here.

## Physics
1. **Gravity & support.** Every object rests on a surface, hangs from a mounted fixture,
   or is visibly held. Nothing floats.
   **Partial support counts as floating.** Check *every* end and edge of *every* object:
   a pencil, spoon, stick, ruler, card or board that is on a surface at one end must
   be supported at the other too (or clearly lie wholly on the surface, or be propped/held).
   An object overhanging an edge must have its centre of mass over the support, and the
   overhanging part must not sit at the upper surface's height over empty space. When an
   object lies on another (book on table, cup on saucer), it must lie *within* the lower
   object's outline. Items drawn *on* another object (labels, decals)
   must read as printed on it, never as a separate floating thing.
2. **Contact.** Feet, bases and edges touch their surface; add a contact shadow.
3. **Ropes, chains, threads & cables.** Anchored at both ends to something real (or exit
   the frame); hang vertically under a weight unless swinging; sag between two anchors.
4. **Angles & perspective.** Flat things (coins, plates, paper, lids) lie flat on the
   ground plane, drawn as ellipses consistent with the scene's eye level. Everything in
   a scene shares one ground plane / horizon. Nothing tilts unless something tilts it.
5. **Mechanisms work.** Any device must be buildable and operable as drawn: a slingshot
   has a frame, an elastic anchored to both arms and a pouch pulled back by someone; a
   grappling hook has a complete hook with its line tied on; a lever has a fulcrum; a
   cart has wheels on axles that touch the ground.
6. **Fluids & particles.** Drips/drool fall down, steam and smoke rise, crumbs lie on surfaces.

## Completeness
7. **No half-finished objects.** Every object is drawn completely, or is cropped by the
   frame edge in an obviously deliberate way (it clearly continues beyond the frame, e.g.
   a table leg going off the bottom). No object is cut off mid-shape inside the frame,
   and nothing important is clipped by the viewBox.
8. **Detail.** Scenes have supporting detail appropriate to the place (a kitchen has
   tiles, fixtures, everyday objects) — but never clutter that competes with the subject.

## Anatomy
9. **Characters, people & animals:** correct counts of eyes (both visible when the face
   is frontal), ears, arms, legs, fingers and tails for what the character is; hands have
   5 fingers (or the character's established count) with the thumb on the correct side;
   limbs join the body where they should and bend the right way at the joints.
10. **Consistent designs.** A recurring character keeps one design everywhere it appears:
    the same silhouette, proportions, colours, clothing, markings and features (and the
    same counts of eyes, limbs and fingers). Keep a reference sheet per recurring
    character and check every new piece against it.
11. **Cats are cats.** Four legs, one tail, two ears, whiskers on both sides of the muzzle;
    paws have toe beans, not fingers; a cat's pose must be one a real cat can hold (check
    the spine, shoulder blades and how the hind legs fold). Each gang member keeps the same
    coat, markings, eye colour and any scars or collars everywhere.
    **Original characters only:** never echo famous cartoon or film cats (Garfield, Tom,
    Top Cat, Puss in Boots' film design, the Aristocats, Hello Kitty and the like).
    **One silhouette, no seams** (the author, 2026-10-21): a side-view cat or dog is one
    continuous outline from nose to tail; no line crosses the body where a leg, the neck or
    the tail joins it, so it never looks like a jointed animatronic. Check every animal at
    100% zoom for joins at the shoulders and hips. A running or leaping cat stretches its
    legs and tail, not its body: the body stays about 2.5–3 heads long.
    **Sides:** every one-sided mark or piece of gear (a notched ear, a sock, a patch, a stud,
    a hoop, a tattoo) is on the cat's own correct side for the way it faces: facing right a
    cat shows its right side, facing left its left side; from the front its left is on the
    viewer's right (README, "The gang's gear").
12. **Scale:** consistent within a scene and across the book (a character is the same size
    next to the same furniture everywhere; README "Scale"). No visible part of a person is
    taller than a door; a kitten is smaller than an adult; a large cat or dog is clearly larger.

## Colour & legibility
13. Palette only (`book/art/README.md`).
    **Dark subjects on dark grounds need separation:** a rim light, a lighter backdrop, or a
    light outline so the silhouette reads in print.
14. Danger colours are for danger; keep the meaning of each accent colour consistent
    across the book: red-orange is Broom, pink is Cuddle, amber is light.

## Text
15. No garbled pseudo-text; squiggles may stand in for writing. Real words only where they
    belong (a van's livery, a label, the gang's throw-ups), and always whole: never a word
    cut by the frame or half hidden so that a fragment reads on its own. Every piece of text
    is set in the book's faces (Alegreya Sans, Fraunces, the spray-paint face), never a
    generic family alone; map labels are at least 7pt in print.

## Tone and the street
16. **Comical and light** (the author, 2026-10-21): the gang are thugs played for laughs —
    tough looks that don't quite work, swagger, slapstick, daft tattoos, too-big kerchiefs,
    too-heavy bling. Nothing menacing, nothing gory; the chasers are comic too.
17. **Graffiti outdoors only**, in the allowed forms and colours (README "Graffiti"): never
    inside a home, never in Broom red-orange or Cuddle pink, only the words MEOW, 9 LIVES and NLG.

## How to audit (mandatory method)
For each piece, write an **object table**: every object (including small props, crumbs,
tools, furniture and each character's feet), its contact point(s), and what is directly
underneath or holding each contact point. Any row where an end, edge or foot has nothing
under it — or where the object sits outside the outline of what supports it — is a defect.
Add a **character row** for each figure: eyes, ears, arms, legs, fingers per hand, tail —
counted, not glanced at — and, for a recurring character, a check against its reference
(every mark and piece of gear, on the correct side for the way it faces).
Looking at the picture as a whole is not enough; the mistakes the author found were all
small props at the edges of scenes.
