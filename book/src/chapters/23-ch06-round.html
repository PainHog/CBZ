Chapter 6

A Round in the House

A robbery is played in rounds. In each round every cat gets a turn, and then the house takes one: the humans wander, the dog sniffs about, and the prize gets closer to being eaten, played with or thrown out.

A round

  1. The Prize Clock. If this round is on the house’s Prize Clock, the human puts the prize in its place and stays in that room this round (unless a noise draws them away). If the prize isn’t where the Clock last put it — and a prize the cats are holding never is — the human goes there instead, stays, and finds it gone at once: Broom +1, and they start Searching (if they weren’t already).
  2. Temptation. The Storyteller may tempt one cat (Chapter 7). If it gives in, it goes first this round.
  3. Cats. Every free cat in the house takes a turn, in any order you like: one Move and one Action, either way round. Cats outside can come in.
  4. The house. Pets, then noise, then the humans’ routines and anyone holding a cat, then Finding it.

Your turn: a Move and an Action

A Move takes you into the next room through an open way, or in or out through an entrance. Walking is never a roll.

An Action is one roll, or one simple thing. Simple things need no roll when no human can see you: pick something up, put it down, hide, or make a noise on purpose (that’s a noise whatever you roll). Seen, any of them is a roll (Chapter 5). Helping another cat’s roll is also your Action, but never a roll. Eating a portion of the prize is always a roll (Chapter 8).

Opening a closed door is an Action (the house says how hard). Going through an entrance that needs a roll takes your Move and your Action. Going out through one that needs no roll can be your Move or your Action, so a cat can walk to the cat flap and out in one turn. A cat can’t go out and come back in on the same turn. An entrance’s roll is made in the doorway: humans in the room it leads into see it.

Doors. Humans and pets pass closed doors as if they were open (a dog noses through behind somebody) and leave them as they found them. Noise carries through them.

Noise

A Scrape or a Flop makes noise. So do noisy entrances such as cat flaps, barking dogs, squawking parrots, and a cat who knocks something over on purpose. In the house’s turn, each noise draws the closest awake human within two rooms who isn’t already in that room and isn’t holding a cat. They go straight there, see only that room, and stay there instead of following their routine. Deal with noises in the order they happened; several noises in one room count as one; each human answers only one; the Storyteller breaks ties.

Luring. Making a noise on purpose is a good way to pull a human out of the room you want — even the one who just put the prize down. But a noise made on purpose leaves a mess, and a human who ends the round in that room finds it: Broom +1.

Distract

A Sweet roll aimed at a human in your room. If it works, that human is Distracted until the house’s turn starts: they see nobody, so the rest of the gang can work right under their nose. It adds Cuddle steps as usual. Each earlier distraction of that human that worked this robbery costs a die. When the house’s turn comes they look round again — and if the prize has been touched, they’ll notice.

The house’s turn

  1. Pets. A sleeping dog wakes if there was noise in its room or next door this round. An awake dog with a cat in its room barks (a noise) and stays put; otherwise the Storyteller rolls its routine (Chapter 10).
  2. Noise, as above.
  3. Routines. The Storyteller rolls a d6 for each other human, reads their routine, and moves them straight to that room. They only see the room they end up in. A human who is Searching doesn’t follow their routine (below).
  4. Holders roll to see what happens to the cat they’re holding (Chapter 9).
  5. Finding it (below).

Finding it

At the end of the house’s turn, in this order:

  • Spotted. If a Searching human (not holding a cat) can see a cat: Broom +1 (once a round, however many).
  • The prize. The prize is disturbed if a step has been done, a portion eaten, or it isn’t where the Clock last put it. Every awake human who isn’t Searching yet or holding a cat, and is in the room the prize is in, or in the room where the Clock put it, finds it: Broom +1 (once, however many) and they start Searching.
  • Messes. A human in a room with a mess finds it: Broom +1, and the mess is cleared.

Searching

A Searching human knows something is up, and stays that way for the rest of the robbery. In the house’s turn, instead of rolling their routine, they:

  1. while the prize is still inside: stay put if it’s in their room, or step into its room if it’s next door;
  2. otherwise stay put if they can see a cat, or step into a next room where a cat isn’t hiding (a cat shut in the Den doesn’t count);
  3. otherwise roll their routine as usual.

So once a human is Searching, don’t let them catch you standing about: hide, keep moving, or keep the prize away from them.

Keep it moving

Most turns are short: “I go to the kitchen and hide under the table.” Let players plan together, but when a round drags, start the house’s turn — humans don’t wait.
