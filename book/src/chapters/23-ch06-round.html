Chapter 6

A Round in the House

A robbery is played in rounds. In each round every cat gets a turn, and then the house takes one: the humans wander, the dog sniffs about, and the prize gets closer to being eaten, played with or thrown out. It gets loud. It gets messy. That’s the job.

A round

  1. The Prize Clock. If this round is on the house’s Prize Clock, the human puts the prize in its place and stays with it this round — they answer no noise until the next round. If the prize isn’t where the Clock last put it — and a prize the cats are holding never is — the human goes to where the Clock last put it instead, stays, and finds it gone at once: Broom +1, and they start Searching (if they weren’t already).
  2. Temptation. The Storyteller may tempt one cat (Chapter 7). If it gives in, it goes first this round.
  3. Cats. Every cat takes a turn, in any order you like: one Move and one Action, either way round. Cats outside can come in. Held and shut-in cats can only try to get free (Chapter 10).
  4. The house. Pets, then noise, then the humans’ routines and anyone holding a cat, then Finding it.

Your turn: a Move and an Action

A Move takes you into the next room through an open way, or in or out through an entrance. Walking is never a roll.

An Action is one roll, or one simple thing. Simple things need no roll when no human can see you: pick something up, put it down, hide, or make a noise on purpose (that’s a noise whatever you roll). Seen, any of them is a roll (Chapter 5). Helping another cat’s roll is also your Action, but never a roll. Eating a portion of the prize is always a roll (Chapter 8).

Opening a closed door is an Action (the house says how hard). Going through an entrance that needs a roll takes your Move and your Action. Going out through one that needs no roll can be your Move or your Action, so a cat can walk to the cat flap and out in one turn. A cat can’t go out and come back in on the same turn. An entrance’s roll is made in the doorway: humans in the room it leads into see it. A roll to open a door inside the house is seen from the room the cat is in.

Doors. Humans and pets pass closed doors as if they were open (a dog noses through behind somebody) and leave them as they found them. Noise carries through them.

Noise

A Scrape or a Flop makes noise. So do noisy entrances such as cat flaps, barking dogs, squawking parrots, and a cat who knocks something over on purpose. In the house’s turn, each noise draws the closest awake human within two rooms (the next room, or the one beyond it) who isn’t already in that room, isn’t holding a cat, and isn’t staying put this round (with the prize the Clock just moved, or by the entrance they just shooed a cat out of). They go straight there, see only that room, and stay there instead of following their routine. Deal with noises in the order they happened; several noises in one room count as one; each human answers only one. If two humans are as close, the one with the higher Attention goes; if that’s tied too, the Storyteller picks.

Luring. Making a noise on purpose is a good way to pull a human out of the room you want — but not the one who has just put the prize down: they stay with it that round. A noise made on purpose leaves a mess (one per room, however many noises), and a human who ends the round in that room finds it: Broom +1. A mess is found once, then it’s cleared.

Distract

A Sweet roll aimed at a human in your room. If it works, that human is Distracted until the house’s turn starts: they see nobody, so the rest of the gang can work right under their nose. It adds Cuddle steps as usual (if they fill Cuddle, the Scoop wins and the human isn’t Distracted: Chapter 5). Each earlier distraction of that human that worked this robbery costs a die — a Lap Cat’s fuss (Chapter 7) counts as one. When the house’s turn comes they look round again — and if the prize has been touched and they’re still in its room when that turn ends, they’ll notice (Finding it).

The house’s turn

  1. Pets. A sleeping dog wakes if there was noise in its room or next door this round. An awake dog with a cat in its room barks (a noise) and stays put — unless the gang has befriended it; otherwise the Storyteller rolls its routine (Chapter 11). A dog that walks into a cat’s room on its routine barks from next turn. A parrot that saw a cat that wasn’t hidden at any time this round squawks (a noise).
  2. Noise, as above.
  3. Routines. The Storyteller rolls a d6 for each other human, reads their routine, and moves them straight to that room. They only see the room they end up in. A human who is Searching doesn’t follow their routine (below).
  4. Holders roll to see what happens to the cat they’re holding (Chapter 10).
  5. Finding it (below).

Finding it

At the end of the house’s turn, in this order:

  • Spotted. If a Searching human (not holding a cat) can see a cat: Broom +1 (once a round, however many).
  • The prize. The prize is disturbed if a step has been done, a portion eaten, or it isn’t where the Clock last put it (an empty spot counts, even once the prize is outside or all eaten). Every awake human who isn’t Searching yet or holding a cat, and is in the room the prize is in, or in the room where the Clock put it, finds it: Broom +1 (once, however many) and they start Searching.
  • Messes. A human in a room with a mess finds it: Broom +1, and the mess is cleared.

Searching

A Searching human knows something is up, and stays that way for the rest of the robbery. In the house’s turn, instead of rolling their routine, they:

  1. while the prize is still inside (a prize that’s all eaten isn’t): stay put if it’s in their room, or step into its room if it’s next door (a Searching human still answers noise first);
  2. otherwise stay put if they can see a cat, or step into a next room where a cat isn’t hiding (a cat shut in the Den doesn’t count);
  3. otherwise roll their routine as usual.

So once a human is Searching, don’t let them catch you standing about: hide, keep moving, or keep the prize away from them.

Keep it moving

Most turns are short: “I leg it to the kitchen and hide under the table.” Let the crew argue about the plan, but when a round drags, start the house’s turn — humans don’t wait.
