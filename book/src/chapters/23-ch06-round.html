Chapter 6

A Round in the House

A robbery is played in rounds. In each round every cat gets a turn, and then the house takes one: the humans wander, the dog sniffs about, and the prize gets closer to being eaten, played with or thrown out.

A round

  1. The Prize Clock. If this round is on the house’s Prize Clock, the human puts the prize in its place and stays in that room this round.
  2. Temptation. The Storyteller may tempt one cat (Chapter 7).
  3. Cats. Every free cat in the house takes a turn, in any order you like: one Move and one Action, either way round. Cats outside can come in.
  4. The house. Noise, then every human’s and pet’s routine, then anyone holding a cat rolls.

Your turn: a Move and an Action

A Move takes you into the next room through an open way, or in or out through an entrance. Walking is never a roll.

An Action is one roll, or one simple thing. Simple things need no roll when no human can see you: pick something up, put it down, eat a portion, help another cat, hide, or make a noise on purpose. Seen, any of them is a roll (Chapter 5).

Opening a closed door is an Action (the house says how hard). Going through an entrance that needs a roll takes your Move and your Action.

Noise

A Scrape or a Flop makes noise. So do cat flaps, barking dogs, squawking parrots, and a cat who knocks something over on purpose. At the end of the round, each noise draws the closest awake human within two rooms who isn’t already in that room. They walk in and stay there for the rest of the round instead of following their routine. Deal with noises in the order they happened; each human answers only one; the Storyteller breaks ties.

Luring. Making a noise on purpose is a good way to pull a human out of the room you want. But if they arrive and find a mess, Broom goes up by 1.

Distract

A Sweet roll aimed at a human who can see you. If it works, that human is Distracted until the end of the round: they see nobody, so the rest of the gang can work right under their nose. It adds Cuddle steps as usual. Each time you distract the same human again in the same robbery, it costs a die.

The house’s turn

  1. Noise, as above.
  2. Routines. The Storyteller rolls a d6 for each other human and pet, reads their routine, and moves them straight to that room. They only see the room they end up in. A human who is Searching doesn’t follow their routine (Chapter 10).
  3. Discovery. Anyone who walked into the prize’s room and found it disturbed adds +1 Broom — and starts Searching.
  4. Holders roll to see what happens to the cat they’re holding (Chapter 9).

Keep it moving

Most turns are short: “I go to the kitchen and hide under the table.” Let players plan together, but when a round drags, start the house’s turn — humans don’t wait.
