Chapter 9

Caught!

“Who’s a good kitty? Who’s a lovely good kitty? Are you hungry? You can stay here with me.” — the end of many a street cat’s career

Getting chased out with a broom is undignified, but you can always come back in another way. Getting picked up is worse. Humans who pick up cats tend to keep them.

Held

When Cuddle fills, a human picks the cat up. A Held cat can’t move, and drops anything it was carrying. While the human is holding a cat, they see nobody else and don’t follow their routine — so a held cat is, in a way, the best distraction there is.

On its turn a held cat can wriggle free (Savage, Tricky; Chonky cats get +1 die). If it works, the cat drops to the floor in that room, seen. Or it can go limp and purr, and let the gang get on with it.

At the end of each round, the human holding a cat rolls a d6:

d6What the human does
1–3Keeps cuddling.
4–5Carries the cat to the Den — the house’s spare room or bathroom — and shuts it in, “just until we find your owner”.
6Puts the cat outside: “Go on, home you go.”

Shut in

A cat in the Den is Shut In; its door stays shut while a cat is inside. A friend in the next room can open it (Streetwise, Tricky — jump for the handle). The cat on its own needs Streetwise, Hard. Once the door is open, the cat is free.

Adopted

Any cat still inside when the robbery ends — held, shut in or free — is adopted. The humans keep it. It gets a name it doesn’t like, a bowl with its picture on, and a warm spot by the radiator. For the gang it’s a loss; for the cat, honestly, it isn’t so bad.

In a one-off robbery, an adopted cat’s player can play a resident for the rest of the session if the Storyteller likes — the house cat, perhaps, who isn’t keen on the new arrival. In a campaign, read on.

Nine Lives

Every cat has 9 Lives. They only matter in a campaign (Chapter 13). When a cat is adopted, its player can spend a Life: the cat slips out of a window in the night and is back with the gang by the next robbery. With no Lives left, the cat stays where it is, happily, for ever.

Nobody gets hurt

Describe every catch as cartoon slapstick: the broom sweeps, the cat skids out of the door and lands on its feet; the scoop is all “ooh, look at you”. The worst thing that can happen is love.
