Chapter 9

The Getaway

“It’s the Five-O! Leg it! LEG IT!” — Pip, every single time

Getting the loot is half the job. Getting it home is the other half: over the fences, dropping bits as you go, while the Five-O — the animal control warden, a harried human with a net and a van — puffs along behind. Get pinched and it’s the pound.

When it starts

When the robbery in the house ends (Chapter 8), the getaway starts. Any cat still inside — held, shut in or free — is pinched: the humans have called animal control, and the warden collects it when the van pulls up. Every other cat is outside, and the whole gang runs for it. No loot to carry? Still run for it (a cornered cat can be pinched, and there’s Cred to win), but keep it quick.

The route

Every house has a getaway route: four obstacles between its garden and the gang’s hideout. Part Four prints one for each house; for your own, roll on the table below. It has five spaces: the garden (where the cats start and the van pulls up), past 1, past 2, past 3 and the hideout. A cat’s next obstacle is the one in front of its space.

The loot

Whatever the gang got out of the house is loot, and it comes in pieces. Food comes in its portions (whatever wasn’t eaten inside). Anything else is 1 piece if Small, 2 if Medium, 3 if Large: the box, and whatever was in it. Portions eaten in the house are already safe — the best place to hide food is inside a cat.

Share the pieces out: each cat carries one piece, a Chonky cat two. Pieces nobody can carry are left in the garden.

A getaway round

  1. Cats. Each cat takes a turn, in any order. It tries to get over the next obstacle (a roll, as the route says), helps a cat on its space (one leads and rolls, up to two help, and the helpers go over with it), picks up a piece of loot dropped on its space, or waits where it is (no roll).
    • It works: over you go, onto the next space, helpers and all. Over the fourth, you’re home with whatever you carry.
    • A Flop: you’re all stuck there this round, and every carrier in the group drops a piece on that space.
  2. The warden. At the end of round 2 the van pulls up on the garden and the warden jumps out (round 3 if nobody was Shooed or Scooped tonight: the humans took their time calling). At the end of every round after that he moves 1 space — 2 if any cat’s roll ended in a Flop this round (not one saved by a Push): the racket gives you away. He never passes a cat: he stops on the space of the rearmost cat still running.
  3. Cornered. A cat on the warden’s space at the end of the round must dodge: Sneaky (slip between his boots), Savage (bolt) or Sweet (“I’m somebody’s cat!”), plain. It works: the net swishes past and the warden goes sprawling over a bin; the cat stays where it is (a dodge never carries it over an obstacle), and he doesn’t move next round. A Flop: pinched — in the van, loot and all. A dodge is each cat’s own roll: nobody helps, and loot costs no die. A sprawled warden stays put next round even after a Flop (once, however many cats dodged), and a cat still on his space at the end of that round must dodge again. Any loot lying on his space is gone.
  4. The getaway ends when every cat is home or pinched.

Running with the loot

A group loses 1 die if any cat in it carries loot (a Chonky cat with a single piece doesn’t count; like Tricky, it comes off after the cap of 5), but carrying doesn’t stop a cat helping. Before the roll, carriers can drop a piece on purpose to lose that die; it lies on that space for a friend to grab — if the warden doesn’t get there first. A cat carrying loot has its mouth full: it can’t be Sweet (Chapter 8) unless it drops its loot first, on purpose, as part of that roll.

Nobody’s watching from a window out here, so getaway rolls fill no meters. A Push still works, paid with Cattitude only; a Purrfect still gives 1 Cattitude, and one you Pushed for still counts for Cred. Knacks count as usual: a Climber for fences and walls, a Hunter for bolting (a Savage crossing or dodge), a Box Sitter for hiding, a Lap Cat for charming. A pet on the route takes its rating off the roll as usual (Chapter 11): every cat or group rolls to get past it, and beating it only gets that group past. A Flop there is like any Flop: stuck, not chased back.

Pinched

A pinched cat goes to the pound. In a one-off robbery its night is over; in a campaign it can spend a Life to bust out (Chapters 10 and 14).

How did it go?

Count against the whole prize (all its portions, or all its pieces): what made it home, plus any portions eaten in the house.

OutcomeWhen
Clean GetawayAll of the prize is home or eaten, and nobody was pinched.
Messy GetawayAt least half of it (round up) — or all of it, but somebody was pinched.

Cred: showing off

Any bunch of cats can nick a chicken. A gang leaves its mark. Showing off scores Cred for the cat who did it; keep it on your cat sheet. Cred never changes how the night went, only who gets to brag about it.

Tagging is your Action. Unseen, it’s a simple thing: no roll, no noise. In view it’s a roll, Sneaky or Savage, plain; if it works, that’s the extra Cred, even on a Sneaky Clean (they saw the mark appear and never saw who did it). A tag nobody saw is a mess: a human who ends a round in that room finds it (+1 Broom, Chapter 6). That cleans up the mess, not the Cred, and the room stays tagged. A tag made in view isn’t a mess (they already know; the roll’s Mood step is the price). Smart gangs tag on the way out.

The catnip stash. Every house has one, and the house says where. It tempts every cat in its room, whatever its instincts (it’s still the Storyteller’s one temptation a round); giving in works like a Curious cat rummaging (a noise, and +1 Broom if a human sees). Or a cat goes for it on purpose, as its Action (in view, a Sneaky or Savage roll, plain). Either way the cat rolls about in it, yowling (a noise, not a mess), and scores 1 Cred (that one isn’t doubled). There’s one stash, so only one cat gets it, and then it tempts nobody. That cat gets the zoomies, which wear off when the getaway starts, even for a cat that’s already outside:

  • +1 die on every roll (still no more than 5);
  • every roll it makes is a noise, even a Clean (a Sneaky Clean still adds nothing to the Mood: they heard a yowl and saw nothing);
  • a tag is always a roll (Sneaky or Savage, plain), seen or not;
  • it can’t resist a temptation, and everything it scores counts double.

The rivals. Every house is on some other gang’s patch, and the house names them. They’ve tagged one room, and tagging over theirs is worth a point more. On the getaway they hang about on one obstacle. Any loot dropped by a cat trying to get over it is nicked at once (gone), and the cat who leads a group over it with a Clean or a Purrfect scores 1 (on top of a Purrfect’s leap). That showed them.

The Cat’s Whiskers. At the end of the night, the cat with the most Cred is the Cat’s Whiskers: bragging rights, the warm spot on the boiler, and the last word on which house is next. Ties share it, and argue. In a campaign, a night when the gang’s Cred adds up to 8 or more, and that wasn’t Busted, is worth 1 Rep (Chapter 14).

Make it a chase

This is the loud bit. Slam the van doors. Have the warden yell “Oi!” When a cat Flops, the chicken leg bounces into the gutter. When the last cat drops into the hideout, let the table cheer.
