Chapter 9

The Getaway

“It’s the Five-O! Leg it! LEG IT!” — Pip, every single time

Getting the loot is half the job. Getting it home is the other half: over the back fence with a chicken leg in your teeth, through next door’s garden, along the alley wall and across the road, dropping bits as you go — with the animal control van rattling up behind you. The gang calls the warden the Five-O: a harried human with a net, a van and a very long list. Get pinched and it’s the pound.

When it starts

When the robbery in the house ends (Chapter 8), the getaway starts. Any cat still inside — held, shut in or free — is pinched: the humans have called animal control, and the warden collects it at the door. Every other cat is outside, and the whole gang runs for it.

The route

Every house has a getaway route: four obstacles between its garden and the gang’s hideout. Part Four prints one for each house; for a house of your own, roll on the table at the end of this chapter. Put a counter for each cat at the start, and one for the warden beside the house.

The loot

Whatever the gang got out of the house is loot, and it comes in pieces. Food comes in its portions (whatever wasn’t eaten inside). Anything else is 1 piece if it’s Small, 2 if it’s Medium and 3 if it’s Large: the box, and whatever was in it. Portions eaten in the house are already safe — the best place to hide food is inside a cat.

Share the pieces out: each cat carries one piece, a Chonky cat two. Pieces nobody can carry are left in the garden.

A getaway round

  1. Cats. Each cat takes a turn, in any order. It tries to get over the next obstacle (a roll, as the route says) — or helps a cat on its space (one leads and rolls, up to two help, and the helpers go over with it), or picks up a piece of loot dropped on its space instead of going on.
    • It works: over you go, onto the next space, helpers and all. Past the fourth obstacle is the hideout: you’re home, with whatever you carry.
    • A Flop: you’re all stuck there this round, and every carrier in the group drops a piece on that space.
  2. The warden. At the end of the first round the van pulls up at the house, and the warden jumps out (at the end of the second round if nobody was Shooed or Scooped tonight: the humans took their time calling). At the end of every round after that, the warden moves one space along the route — two if any cat Flopped this round. The racket gives you away.
  3. Cornered. Any cat on the warden’s space, or behind it, must dodge: Sneaky (slip between their boots), Savage (bolt) or Sweet (“I’m somebody’s cat!”), plain. It works: it bolts on to the space just ahead of the warden. A Flop: pinched — it goes in the van, loot and all. Loot lying on the warden’s space, or behind it, is gone too.
  4. The getaway ends when every cat is home or pinched.

Running with the loot

A cat carrying loot loses 1 die on its getaway rolls — a Chonky cat carrying a single piece doesn’t. Before you roll you can drop a piece on purpose to lose that die. It lies on that space for a friend to grab — if the warden doesn’t get there first.

Nobody’s watching from a window out here, so getaway rolls fill no meters. A Push still works, paid with Cattitude only. Knacks count as usual: a Climber for fences and walls, a Hunter for bolting, a Box Sitter for hiding, a Lap Cat for charming. An obstacle with a dog or a cat on it lists its rating, and that takes dice off the roll as usual (Chapter 11).

Pinched

A pinched cat goes to the pound. In a one-off robbery its night is over. In a campaign it can spend a Life to bust out before the next robbery (Chapters 10 and 14).

How did it go?

Count the loot: the pieces that made it home, plus any portions eaten in the house.

OutcomeWhen
Clean GetawayAll of the loot is home or eaten, and nobody was pinched.
Messy GetawayAt least half of it — or all of it, but somebody was pinched.
BustedLess than half.

A route for your own house

Roll a d6 four times, or pick. An Easy house has plain obstacles; a Hard one has two or three Tricky ones.

d6ObstacleRoll
1A garden fenceSavage or Sneaky, plain (a Climber’s Knack)
2A hedge, or a row of binsSneaky or Savage, plain
3A high wallSavage or Sneaky, Tricky (a Climber’s Knack)
4A yard dograting 1–2: Savage drives it off, Sneaky slips past, Sweet befriends a soft one
5A greenhouse or a shed roofSneaky or Streetwise, Tricky
6A roadStreetwise or Savage, plain or Tricky (a Hunter’s Knack to bolt)

Make it a chase

This is the loud bit. Slam the van doors. Have the warden huff, puff and shout “Come back here, you lot!” When a cat Flops, the chicken leg goes bouncing into the gutter and the net swishes past an ear. A pinched cat rides off in the van, sulking. When the last cat drops into the hideout, let the table cheer.
