Chapter 12

The Storyteller

You are the house. You are the grown-up with the chicken, the kid who wants a kitten, the dog who has never once caught anything, the clock on the wall — and, at the end, the Five-O, puffing after the gang with a net. You don’t try to beat the gang — the dice and the routines do that. Your job is to make the house feel alive, loud and fair.

Three promises

  • Play the routines straight. Roll every human’s and pet’s routine in the open and do what the die says. The players should be able to plan around the house, and be surprised by it.
  • Say the stakes before the roll. “That’s Tricky, and Mr Pell is watching, so it fills Broom if he sees it.” Nobody should be surprised by the rules.
  • Make it funny, never cruel. Cats skid, slide, sneeze into the flour and crash into the recycling. Humans say “aww” and “HEY!”. The tough talk is all talk, and nobody gets hurt (Chapter 10).

Before you start

  1. Pick a house from Part Four, or make one (Chapter 13). Read its page and keep its tracker in front of you.
  2. Set out the tracker: Patience (adjusted for gang size, below), the Mood meters at 0, the Prize Clock.
  3. Read the start of the house aloud: the street, the house, the human going in with the prize.
  4. Show the players the house page — the map and its entrances, who lives there, Patience and the Prize Clock. None of it is secret; the dice keep the surprises. They choose where each cat goes in.

Gang size

The houses are written for four cats. With one or two players, each player runs two cats, so the gang has at least three. For your own houses: with three cats, the house’s Patience is 1 higher, the prize goes 2 rounds later, and each cat starts with 3 Cattitude; with five, Patience is 1 lower. Only the “Gone” row of the Prize Clock moves (and Lights Out with it). The ready-to-run houses print their own three- and five-cat numbers in At a glance — a few differ from this rule, because we tested each house — so use those. Food portions already count the cats.

Every round, in order

  1. Prize Clock. Does the prize get put down or moved this round? Its human goes there and stays, deaf to noise this round. (Not where the Clock left it, or in the cats’ paws? The human goes there and finds it gone at once: +1 Broom, and they start Searching.) Is it gone this round?
  2. Tempt one cat, or nobody (never the same cat two rounds running). If it gives in, it goes first.
  3. Cats act. For each roll: who can see it? What does it need? Then update the meters.
  4. Pets: wake sleeping dogs near noise; a dog with a cat in its room barks; a parrot that saw a cat squawks; roll the others’ routines.
  5. Noise: list this round’s noises in order; move the closest human within two rooms to each (not one already there, holding a cat, or staying put this round).
  6. Routines: roll for every other human. Searching humans hunt instead.
  7. Holders roll.
  8. Finding it: a Searching human sees a cat: +1 Broom. Anyone not yet Searching in the disturbed prize’s room, or its Clock room: +1 Broom, and Searching. A mess found: +1 Broom.
  9. Is it Lights Out? Is no free cat inside — and is the prize out, eaten or gone, or has the gang run for it? Then the house is done: anyone still inside is pinched, and the getaway starts.

Every getaway round, in order

  1. Cats: over the next obstacle (a Flop: stuck, and carriers drop a piece), help, or grab a dropped piece.
  2. The warden: arrives at the end of round 1 (round 2 if nobody was Shooed or Scooped tonight); then moves 1 space a round, 2 after a Flop.
  3. Cornered: cats on or behind the warden’s space dodge (plain) or are pinched; loot there is gone.
  4. Everyone home or pinched? Count the loot.

Calling the roll

When a player says what their cat does, decide three things: which approach (they usually tell you), how hard (plain, Tricky or Hard — the house lists the hard bits), and who can see it. Then let them count their dice.

It’s…For example
Plaina low chair, a door already ajar, a bag left open
Tricky (−1)a kitchen counter, a lever handle, a stiff window, foil on a plate
Hard (−2)the top of the fridge, a round doorknob, a drainpipe, a microchip cat flap

Ties. When the rules make you pick between humans — two as close to a noise, two who saw the same roll — the one with the higher Attention goes first. Still tied? Roll a die. Save your own choices for which cat a meter falls on and whom to tempt.

When the meters fill

Make Shoo! and Scooped! big moments. Stop, describe it, then do the bookkeeping: if the caught cat had the prize, put it back where the Clock left it; empty the meter; lower Patience by 1 out loud — the players should feel the house running out of patience — and if the other meter is now at or over Patience, drop it to one below.

Sometimes a gang lets one cat take the broom on purpose — taking one for the crew — so the others can finish the job while the house calms down. That’s allowed, and it’s a good story: the house still loses Patience for good, and the entrance the cat went out of is shut (unless it was the last one open).

Your first robbery

Run Number 12 (Chapter 15). Every round, say out loud where Mr Pell goes and why: a noise, the chicken, or his routine roll. When the chicken is put down, describe the foil crackling. When the first cat flops in front of Mr Pell, have him say “Oi!” (or “Aww!”) and put the 2 steps on the fuller meter — the players learn the Mood in one go.

Young players

For players around eight: let them count their own dice, read the highest die together, and use tokens for Cattitude. Keep the map on the table and the cats on it as counters. Skip the lure details in the first robbery; teach Distract when the Charmer needs it.

How hard are the houses?

We played each house thousands of times with computer gangs that follow the rules exactly. A gang of four sensible players with rolled-up cats gets the prize (a Clean or Messy Getaway) about 9 times in 10 at an Easy house, 4 times in 5 at a Standard house, and a bit more than half the time at a Hard house. The ready-made gang does better, especially at Grandpa Wendell’s. At the Hard houses a Clean Getaway is rare: eating half the cake and getting everyone out is a good night. About one Hard robbery in five ends with a cat adopted — one in four at Chef Marchetti’s. Three or five players do about as well, using each house’s own numbers for the gang’s size.

Questions at the table

  • Jumping for a door handle — is that a Climber’s Knack (jump) or a Curious one (open)? Either.
  • Can cats outside help one coming in? No: a helper is in the same room, and outside isn’t a room.
  • The Clock moves a prize the gang has dropped somewhere else. The human goes to the old spot and finds it gone; the old spot is still “where the Clock last put it”.
  • Does a held cat count as seen? Yes: a Searching human who can see it adds +1 Broom, and a parrot squawks at it. So does a cat being chased through a room.
  • A cat shooed out — can it come straight back? Not this round; next round, through any entrance still open.
  • Somebody’s Cattitude got lost track of. Use tokens, one pile per cat, and keep each cat’s pile on its sheet.
