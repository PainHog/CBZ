Chapter 14

The Block

One job makes a great evening. A street full of houses makes a campaign: the gang gets a name, a reputation, a lot of big talk, and a list of humans who now lock their windows.

Playing a campaign

Each session is one robbery at one house on the Block. Draw a simple map of the street, and add houses as the gang hears about them: the five in Part Four, and any you make.

The gang’s name

A campaign gang needs a name. Roll a d6 or pick: 1 the Bottle Cap Bandits, 2 the Lamppost Mob, 3 the Dustbin Dozen (however many you are), 4 the Midnight Yowlers, 5 the Sticky Paw Syndicate, 6 the Back Fence Crew.

What carries over

ThingBetween robberies
CattitudeBack to the starting amount each robbery: 2 (3 in a gang of three), plus the gang’s Rep bonus.
LivesCarry over. Spend one when a cat is pinched to bust it out of the pound before the next robbery.
RepThe gang gets 1 Rep for every Clean Getaway, and 1 Rep for a night when the gang’s Cred adds up to 8 or more, and that wasn’t Busted (Chapter 9).
HousesA house the gang has robbed before starts with Patience 1 lower (never below 2). They remember.

Rep

At 3, 6 and 9 Rep, every cat in the gang starts each robbery with 1 more Cattitude (never above 5). A cat who joins later gets the gang’s bonus too. A gang with a big Rep is bolder, not stronger — so as the Rep grows, take it to harder houses.

Cats left in the pound

A cat pinched with no Lives to spend leaves the gang — somebody adopts it from the pound — and its player makes a new cat, with 9 Lives of its own. The old one isn’t gone: it lives somewhere on the Block now. One day the gang may rob that very house — and meet a familiar face by the radiator, who might help them, or might not.

A cat’s last Life

When a cat spends its ninth Life, it has nothing left to spend: the next time it’s pinched, it stays in the pound until somebody adopts it. Give it a send-off fit for a legend: tell the tale of its best job one more time, with extra swagger.
