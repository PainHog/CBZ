Chapter 10

Who Lives There

Every house has its people and its pets, and every one of them has a routine. The Storyteller plays them all — fairly, by the dice.

Humans

Each human has an Attention of 0, 1 or 2: how hard they are to fool. When a human is watching, their Attention takes dice away from Sneaky and Sweet rolls (only the highest Attention counts if several are watching). Savage rolls ignore it — but Savage never goes unnoticed.

Each human has a d6 routine: six results, each “stays put” or a room. In the house’s turn the Storyteller rolls for each human who didn’t answer a noise and isn’t Searching, and they go straight to that room. They only see the room they end up in.

Searching

Searching

A human who finds the prize disturbed — beside it at the end of the house’s turn, or sent by the Prize Clock to fetch it — adds +1 Broom and starts Searching for the rest of the robbery (Chapter 6). In the house’s turn, instead of following their routine, a Searching human:

  1. stays put if the prize is in their room, or steps into its room if it’s next door; otherwise
  2. stays put if they can see a cat, or steps into a next room with a cat who isn’t hiding (the Storyteller picks between rooms); otherwise
  3. follows their routine as usual.

At the end of every house’s turn, if a Searching human can see a cat, Broom goes up by 1. A human holding a cat isn’t Searching while they hold it. Hidden cats aren’t seen, so a Searching human walks right past them — but nobody can hide while holding the prize.

Pets

Pets have a rating of 1 to 3 (how many dice they take away when you deal with them) and a d6 routine like a human’s. A routine result of sniff means the pet goes one room towards the nearest cat (ties: the Storyteller picks). Pets don’t fill the Mood; they make noise, and they get in the way. A pet that is driven off hides for 3 rounds, counting this one — put 3 tokens on it and take one off at the end of each round — then it’s back where it started, awake.

Dog “Woof. WOOF. Woof woof.”

Rating
1–3
Routine
d6: rooms, stay, sniff
Asleep
Some dogs start asleep. A sleeping dog does nothing until there’s been noise in its room or the next one; it wakes at the start of the house’s turn and acts at once.
Bark
In the house’s turn, an awake dog with a cat in its room barks (a noise, so a human may come) and stays put. Dogs smell hidden cats.
Charge
A cat that walks into an awake dog’s room, or starts its turn there, must leave or deal with the dog before doing anything else; helping the cat who deals with it counts. A cat with no Action left (it climbed in) deals with it first thing next turn. A cat that ends its turn there without dealing with it is chased back the way it came (outside again, if it just came in). Hauling the prize out counts as leaving.
Beat it
The roll loses dice equal to the dog’s rating. Savage drives it off (it hides for 3 rounds, counting this one). Sweet befriends a soft dog: it leaves the whole gang alone for the rest of the robbery. Sneaky slips past it for this turn. A Flop: the dog chases you back the way you came (that’s not your Move; a carrier drops the prize).
