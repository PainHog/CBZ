Chapter 11

The Storyteller

You are the house. You are the grown-up with the chicken, the kid who wants a kitten, the dog who has never once caught anything, and the clock on the wall. You don’t try to beat the gang — the dice and the routines do that. Your job is to make the house feel alive and fair.

Three promises

  • Play the routines straight. Roll every human’s and pet’s routine in the open and do what the die says. The players should be able to plan around the house, and be surprised by it.
  • Say the stakes before the roll. “That’s Tricky, and Mr Pell is watching, so it fills Broom if he sees it.” Nobody should be surprised by the rules.
  • Make it funny, never cruel. Cats skid, slide, sneeze into the flour. Humans say “aww” and “HEY!”. Nobody gets hurt (Chapter 9).

Before you start

  1. Pick a house from Part Four, or make one (Chapter 12). Read its page and keep its tracker in front of you.
  2. Set out the tracker: Patience (adjusted for gang size, below), the Mood meters at 0, the Prize Clock.
  3. Read the start of the house aloud: the street, the house, the human going in with the prize.
  4. Show the players the map’s ways in. They choose where each cat goes.

Gang size

The houses are written for four cats (a gang of one or two plays as three). With three, the house’s Patience is 1 higher, the prize goes 2 rounds later, and each cat starts with 3 Cattitude. With five, Patience is 1 lower and the prize goes 1 round sooner. Food portions already count the cats.

Every round, in order

  1. Prize Clock. Does the prize get put down or moved this round? Its human goes there and stays. (Not where the Clock left it, or in the cats’ paws? The human goes there and finds it gone at once: +1 Broom, and they start Searching.) Is it gone this round?
  2. Tempt one cat, or nobody (never the same cat two rounds running). If it gives in, it goes first.
  3. Cats act. For each roll: who can see it? What does it need? Then update the meters.
  4. Pets: wake sleeping dogs near noise; a dog with a cat in its room barks; roll the others’ routines.
  5. Noise: list this round’s noises in order; move the closest human within two rooms (not already there) to each.
  6. Routines: roll for every other human. Searching humans hunt instead.
  7. Holders roll.
  8. Finding it: a Searching human sees a cat: +1 Broom. Someone new beside the disturbed prize: +1 Broom, and Searching. A mess found: +1 Broom.
  9. Is it Lights Out? Is any free cat still inside?

Calling the roll

When a player says what their cat does, decide three things: which approach (they usually tell you), how hard (plain, Tricky or Hard — the house lists the hard bits), and who can see it. Then let them count their dice.

It’s…For example
Plaina low chair, a door already ajar, a bag left open
Tricky (−1)a kitchen counter, a lever handle, a stiff window, foil on a plate
Hard (−2)the top of the fridge, a round doorknob, a drainpipe, a microchip cat flap

When the meters fill

Make Shoo! and Scooped! big moments. Stop, describe it, then do the bookkeeping: put the prize back where the Clock left it, empty the meter, and lower Patience by 1 out loud — the players should feel the house running out of patience.

Sometimes a gang lets one cat take the broom on purpose, so the others can finish the job while the house calms down. That’s allowed, and it’s a good story: the house still loses Patience for good, and the entrance the cat went out of is shut.

Young players

For players around eight: let them count their own dice, read the highest die together, and use tokens for Cattitude. Keep the map on the table and the cats on it as counters. Skip the distraction and lure details in the first robbery; they’ll find them by themselves.

How hard are the houses?

We played each house thousands of times with a computer gang that follows the rules exactly. With four sensible players, the gang gets the prize about 9 times in 10 at an Easy house, 3 times in 4 at a Standard house, and a bit more than half the time at a Hard house. At a Hard house, about one robbery in six ends with a cat adopted. Three or five players do about as well, once you adjust the house for the gang’s size.
