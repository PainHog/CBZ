Chapter 1

Welcome to the Street

“She had a chicken. A whole chicken. In a bag. And she went in and shut the door.” — Pip, age nine months

You are a gang of street cats. You live behind the bins, under the cars and on the warm roofs of the bakery. Tonight one of you saw a human carry something into a house: something delicious, something fun, or a box. Now the whole gang wants it. You’ll get it at all costs — without getting caught.

Cat Burglars is a tabletop roleplaying game for 3 to 5 players and one Storyteller, for ages 8 and up. Each player is one cat in the gang. The Storyteller plays the house: the humans who live there, their pets, and the prize that is about to be eaten, played with or put away. One robbery takes about one to two hours.

What you need

  • Six-sided dice — about ten for the table. A roll never uses more than five.
  • An eight-sided die (d8) for making cats. No d8? Roll a d6 twice and use the table’s note.
  • A cat sheet for each player and the house tracker for the Storyteller (both at the back of this book), and pencils.
  • A handful of tokens (coins, buttons, cat treats) for Cattitude.
  • The house you’ll rob. Part Four has five ready to play.

How a robbery goes

  1. Make your cats (Chapter 2), or pick from the ready-made gang (Chapter 3).
  2. The human goes in. The Storyteller reads the start of the house: who lives there, and what the human just carried inside.
  3. Pick your ways in. Every house has several entrances — a cat flap, a window left open, the gap behind the human’s legs. Cats can split up.
  4. Play in rounds. In each round every cat gets one Move and one Action. Then the house moves: the humans wander, the dog sniffs about, and the Prize Clock ticks.
  5. Get it, and get out. The prize must end up eaten or outside, and every cat must end up outside too.
  6. Find out how it went: a Clean Getaway, a Messy Getaway, or Busted.

The whole game on one hand

To do something tricky, roll some six-sided dice and look at the highest one. A 6 means it works. A 4 or 5 means it works, but you made a noise. A 1, 2 or 3 means it didn’t work, and you made a noise. Two 6s is Purrfect.

Being seen isn’t being caught. Being seen doing something is. Every time a human sees you do something, their mood fills up — towards the Broom if you’re naughty, towards a Cuddle if you’re sweet. Fill one all the way and a cat gets chased out or scooped up.

Nobody gets hurt

This is a cartoon. Cats always land on their feet, dogs only ever bark and chase, and the broom only ever sweeps a cat out of the door. The worst thing that can happen to a cat in this game is that a human loves it so much they keep it — and it has to live in a warm house with a full food bowl for ever. The gang will miss it terribly.

For the Storyteller

Part Two is the rules, Part Three is how to run them, and Part Four is five houses ready to play. Read Chapter 11 before your first robbery; it has a checklist for every round.

Words to know

WordWhat it means
ApproachHow your cat does something: Sneaky, Sweet, Savage or Streetwise. Each has a rating of 1 to 4.
InstinctSomething your cat can’t help doing, like knocking things off tables. It helps you — and tempts you.
CattitudeYour cat’s swagger, kept as tokens. Spend it for an extra die, a reroll, or to resist a temptation.
The MoodHow the household feels about the cats: two meters, Broom and Cuddle.
PatienceHow full a Mood meter can get before something happens.
Prize ClockHow many rounds until the prize is eaten, put away or thrown out.
RoundEvery cat takes a turn, then the house does.
StorytellerThe player who runs the house and everyone in it.

Example of play

Storyteller: “Mr Pell puts the chicken on the kitchen counter and fills the kettle at the sink, humming. The kitchen window is open a crack above the bins.”

Tilly’s player: “Two-Bin Tilly hops up the bins and slips through the window behind his back. Sneaky!” Tilly has Sneaky 2, and climbing is her Knack (she’s a Climber), but the window is Tricky and Mr Pell is right there (Attention 1): 2 + 1 − 1 − 1 = 1 die. She rolls a 6. A Sneaky Clean: she’s in, and he never noticed.

Big Biscuit’s player: “Biscuit goes through the cat flap. It clacks.” No roll, but the cat flap makes a noise. In the house’s turn, Mr Pell goes into the utility room to see what that was — leaving Tilly alone with the chicken…
