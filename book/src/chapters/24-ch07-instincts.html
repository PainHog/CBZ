Chapter 7

Instincts

Cats can’t help themselves. There is a glass on the edge of the table. There is a box. Something moved. Every cat has two instincts: things it’s brilliant at, and things it simply cannot walk past.

Knacks

When what you’re doing suits one of your instincts, roll +1 die. That’s your Knack. You only ever get one Knack die on a roll, even if both instincts fit.

Temptation

Each room in a house lists its temptations — the cake on the side, a basket of washing, a dangling string — and which instincts they tempt. At the start of each round, the Storyteller may tempt one cat who is in a room with a temptation for one of its instincts, naming the cat and the temptation. (Only one cat per round, however big the gang, and never the same cat two rounds running.) Any room with an awake dog or house cat in it tempts Scrappers. The tempted cat decides at once; if it gives in, it takes its turn first this round, before any other cat.

The tempted cat chooses:

  • Give in. Gain 1 Cattitude. Your Action this round is the instinct thing, done right away, in that room, before you move. It is never a roll. If you were carrying the prize, you drop it there first (the other carriers let go too).
  • Resist. Spend 1 Cattitude. If you have none, you must give in.

The Seen? column is what giving in adds to the Mood if a human sees it. Chasing a fly or climbing a bookcase looks like ordinary cat business; eating the dog’s dinner doesn’t.

Why give in?

Giving in costs your Action and makes trouble, but it pays for the Cattitude that makes your important rolls work. A Lap Cat giving in at the right moment is a free distraction; a Box Sitter giving in is a free hiding place. The Storyteller will pick the worst moment to tempt you. Pick the best moment to say yes.

Tempting fairly

Tempt the cat whose temptation makes the best story, but don’t tempt the same player every round. You name the cat and the temptation; if you can’t choose, roll for it among the cats who could be tempted. Late in the robbery, tempt the cat who can’t pay: resisting is cheap for a cat about to lose its Cattitude anyway.
