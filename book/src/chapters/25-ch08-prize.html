Chapter 8

In, Out, and the Prize

You saw it go in. Now you have to get in after it, get it, and get out again — every one of you — before it’s eaten, put away or flattened for the recycling.

Getting in

The robbery starts with every cat outside. Each house has three or four entrances, and its map lists them: where each leads, how big it is, and what it takes to get through.

  • Going through an entrance, in or out, is your Move. If the entrance needs a roll (a climb up the drainpipe, a cat flap to jam open), that roll is your Action too. Going out through one that needs no roll can be your Move or your Action.
  • A noisy entrance (a rattling cat flap) makes a noise every time a cat goes through it.
  • A climb entrance (a window up the drainpipe) takes nothing bigger than Small.
  • Cats can split up and use different entrances.
  • The front door only works while the human is coming in (round 1 in most houses), and only one cat a round gets through before it shuts: slip past their legs (Sneaky, Hard) or say hello (Sweet, plain), in view of the human the house names. A try that fails uses it up for that round.
  • Meow at a door (Sweet) where a human is, and they open it and let you out. Any entrance that is a door counts — the front door in any round, a back, patio or garage door, a door with a cat flap in it — but not a window, a skylight or a climb. Not with your mouth full: a cat carrying something can’t be Sweet.
  • A shut entrance — the window someone closed after a Shoo! — can be opened again from inside (Streetwise, Hard).

Ways through the house

Rooms join by ways: doorways, stairs, gaps. A way is Tight (Lean cats only, and Small things), Normal (any cat; things up to Medium) or Big (anything). A closed door has to be opened first: usually Streetwise, Tricky (jump for the handle) or Savage, Hard (shove).

Where it goes

The prize starts in the human’s arms, and nobody can take it from them. The house’s Prize Clock says which round it’s put down, where, when it’s moved, and the round it’s gone — eaten, put in a drawer, or flattened for the recycling. When the Clock moves it, the human who moves it stays with it for the round, whatever noise the gang makes.

If the prize isn’t where the Clock last put it when the Clock says to move it — and a prize the cats are holding never is, even in that room — the human goes to where the Clock last put it instead, stays, and finds it gone at once: +1 Broom, and they start Searching (if they weren’t already).

Humans notice a prize that’s been touched. At the end of every house’s turn, anyone beside a disturbed prize finds it (Chapter 6, Finding it). Distracting the human by the counter buys you a round of work — but if they’re still there when the house’s turn ends, they’ll notice.

Getting at it

A prize’s spot can take several steps: up on the counter, then the foil off. Each step needs one roll that works, by any cat in the room. A step done stays done for the whole gang. Nobody can pick up or eat the prize until every step is done. Getting the prize down from its spot isn’t climbing.

Each carrier picks it up as its Action. After that they hold it without spending Actions, until they put it down, give in to a temptation, are Shooed or are Scooped. Carriers move together, using their Moves, and a cat holding the prize can’t hide. A cat with its mouth full can’t be Sweet.

Hauling

Moving a Medium or Large prize into another room, or out through an entrance, is a roll every time, seen or not: it’s heavy, it bumps, it scrapes along the floor. A Small prize only needs the roll when a human in either room can see it; moved where nobody sees, the carrier just walks.

  • It is every carrier’s Action for the round. One carrier rolls Sneaky (quietly) or Savage (heave). The other carriers are its helpers, and any other cat in the room may help too (+1 die each, and it’s their Action; still +2 at most); only the carriers move with the prize. It’s plain unless the house says otherwise, and pushing is a Knocker’s Knack. A human in either room — the one it leaves or the one it enters — sees it.
  • If the entrance has its own roll (jam the flap, climb), that is the roll.
  • It works: the carriers move, prize and all, and any noise is in the room it ends up in (a haul out of the house makes its noise in the room it left, like a cat going out through a noisy entrance). A Flop: it doesn’t budge.
  • Hauling out of an awake dog’s room counts as leaving it (the dog takes no dice off the haul); if the haul fails there, the dog chases the carriers back the way they came and they drop the prize where they end up. Hauling into an awake dog’s room uses the carriers’ Actions, so they stay and deal with the dog first thing next turn.
  • If the leader is Shooed or Scooped, the haul fails: the others let go, and the prize goes back where the Clock last put it.

Eating it

Delicious prizes come in portions, set by the house per cat in the gang. Eating one is an Action and always a roll, seen or not: Sneaky to eat it quietly, Savage to wolf it down — a Foodie gets its Knack either way. It’s plain unless the house says otherwise. You can carry food out instead, but eating is usually the surer plan: every haul is a roll too, and a cat carrying the prize can’t hide.

Getting out, and the end

The prize is gone at the start of its Clock’s last round — unless a cat is holding it then, and it stays the gang’s to get out (if they put it down again after that, it’s gone). Lights Out falls at the end of the second round after that: the humans go to bed, and any cat still inside — held, shut in, or free — is adopted.

The robbery ends at Lights Out — or sooner, at the end of a round, if no free cat is inside and either the prize is got or gone, or the gang outside decides to go home. Any cat still inside then is adopted. Don’t go home while a friend is held or shut in: wait for the holder to put it down, or go back in for it (Chapter 9).
