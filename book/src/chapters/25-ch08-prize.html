Chapter 8

In, Out, and the Prize

You saw it go in. Now you have to get in after it, get it, and get out again — every one of you — before it’s eaten, put away or flattened for the recycling.

Getting in

The robbery starts with every cat outside. Each house has three or four entrances, and its map lists them: where each leads, how big it is, and what it takes to get through.

  • Going through an entrance, in or out, is your Move. If the entrance needs a roll (a climb up the drainpipe, a cat flap to jam open), that roll is your Action too.
  • Cats can split up and use different entrances.
  • The front door only works while the human is coming in (round 1 in most houses), and only one cat gets through before it shuts: slip past their legs (Sneaky, Hard, in view of the human) or say hello (Sweet — they let you in).
  • A shut entrance — the window someone closed after a Shoo! — can be opened again from inside (Streetwise, Hard).

Ways through the house

Rooms join by ways: doorways, stairs, gaps. A way is Tight (Lean cats only, and Small things), Normal (any cat; things up to Medium) or Big (anything). A closed door has to be opened first: usually Streetwise, Tricky (jump for the handle) or Savage, Hard (shove).

The prize

Before the robbery the Storyteller decides what the human carried in, or rolls a d6: 1–2 delicious, 3–4 fun, 5–6 a box, then a d6 on that table. The ready-to-run houses in Part Four each have their own.

Where it goes

The prize starts in the human’s arms. The house’s Prize Clock says which round it’s put down, where, when it’s moved, and the round it’s gone — eaten, put in a drawer, or flattened for the recycling. When the Clock moves it, the human who moves it stays in that room for the round.

If the gang already has the prize when the Clock says to move it, the human goes to fetch it, finds it gone (+1 Broom), and starts Searching (Chapter 10).

Getting at it

A prize’s spot can take several steps: up on the counter, then the foil off. Each step needs one roll that works, by any cat in the room. A step done stays done for the whole gang. Getting the prize down from its spot isn’t climbing.

Each carrier picks it up as its Action; then the carriers move together, using their Moves. Moving it past a human is a roll: one carrier leads (Sneaky or Savage) and the others help. A cat with its mouth full can’t be Sweet.

Eating it

Delicious prizes come in portions, set by the house per cat in the gang. Eating one is an Action. You can also carry food out instead.

Getting out, and the end

The prize is gone at the start of its Clock’s last round. Lights Out falls at the end of the second round after that: the humans go to bed, and any cat still inside — held, shut in, or free — is adopted. The robbery ends at Lights Out, or as soon as no free cat is inside.

Gang size

The houses are written for four cats. With three, the house’s Patience is 1 higher and the prize goes 1 round later. With five, it goes 1 round sooner. Food portions already count the cats.
