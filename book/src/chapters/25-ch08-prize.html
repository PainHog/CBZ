Chapter 8

In, Out, and the Prize

You saw it go in. Now you have to get in after it, get it, and get out again — every one of you — before it’s eaten, put away or flattened for the recycling.

Getting in

The robbery starts with every cat outside. Each house has three or four entrances, and its map lists them: where each leads, how big it is, and what it takes to get through.

  • Going through an entrance, in or out, is your Move. If the entrance needs a roll (a climb up the drainpipe, a cat flap to jam open), that roll is your Action too. Going out through one that needs no roll can be your Move or your Action.
  • A noisy entrance (a rattling cat flap) makes a noise every time a cat goes through it.
  • A climb entrance (a window up the drainpipe) takes nothing bigger than Small.
  • Cats can split up and use different entrances.
  • The front door only works while the human is coming in (round 1 in most houses), and only one cat gets through before it shuts: slip past their legs (Sneaky, Hard, in view of the human) or say hello (Sweet — they let you in).
  • A shut entrance — the window someone closed after a Shoo! — can be opened again from inside (Streetwise, Hard).

Ways through the house

Rooms join by ways: doorways, stairs, gaps. A way is Tight (Lean cats only, and Small things), Normal (any cat; things up to Medium) or Big (anything). A closed door has to be opened first: usually Streetwise, Tricky (jump for the handle) or Savage, Hard (shove).

The prize

Before the robbery the Storyteller decides what the human carried in, or rolls a d6: 1–2 delicious, 3–4 fun, 5–6 a box, then a d6 on that table. The ready-to-run houses in Part Four each have their own.

Where it goes

The prize starts in the human’s arms. The house’s Prize Clock says which round it’s put down, where, when it’s moved, and the round it’s gone — eaten, put in a drawer, or flattened for the recycling. When the Clock moves it, the human who moves it stays in that room for the round, unless a noise draws them away.

If the prize isn’t where the Clock last put it when the Clock says to move it, the human goes there instead, stays, and finds it gone: +1 Broom, and they start Searching (if they weren’t already).

Humans notice a prize that’s been touched. At the end of every house’s turn, anyone beside a disturbed prize finds it (Chapter 6, Finding it). Distracting the human by the counter buys you a round of work — not a round of nobody noticing.

Getting at it

A prize’s spot can take several steps: up on the counter, then the foil off. Each step needs one roll that works, by any cat in the room. A step done stays done for the whole gang. Getting the prize down from its spot isn’t climbing.

Each carrier picks it up as its Action. After that they hold it without spending Actions, until they put it down, give in to a temptation, are Shooed or are Scooped. Carriers move together, using their Moves, and a cat holding the prize can’t hide. A cat with its mouth full can’t be Sweet.

Hauling

Moving a Medium or Large prize into another room, or out through an entrance, is a roll every time, seen or not: it’s heavy, it bumps, it scrapes along the floor. A Small prize only needs the roll when a human in either room can see it.

  • It is every carrier’s Action for the round. One carrier rolls Sneaky (quietly) or Savage (heave); the others help (+1 die each).
  • If the entrance has its own roll (jam the flap, climb), that is the roll.
  • It works: everyone moves, prize and all. A Flop: it doesn’t budge. Scrapes and Flops make noise, as always.

Eating it

Delicious prizes come in portions, set by the house per cat in the gang. Eating one is an Action. You can also carry food out instead.

Getting out, and the end

The prize is gone at the start of its Clock’s last round — unless a cat is holding it then, and it stays the gang’s to get out. Lights Out falls at the end of the second round after that: the humans go to bed, and any cat still inside — held, shut in, or free — is adopted. The robbery ends at Lights Out, or as soon as no free cat is inside.

Gang size

The houses are written for four cats. With three, the house’s Patience is 1 higher, the prize goes 2 rounds later, and each cat starts with 3 Cattitude. With five, Patience is 1 lower and the prize goes 1 round sooner. Food portions already count the cats.
