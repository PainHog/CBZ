Chapter 12

Making a House

Every house on the street has a story: who lives there, what they just brought home, and why they left the kitchen window open. Here is how to build one that plays well.

A house needs

  • 5 to 8 rooms, with a map. Each room: its name, any hiding spots that are missing, and one or two temptations (with the instinct they tempt).
  • Ways between rooms: their size, and any closed doors with how hard they are to open.
  • 3 or 4 entrances — never fewer than two — each with its room, size and what it takes. At least one should need no climbing, so a Medium or Large prize can get out.
  • A Den: a room with a door, away from the entrances.
  • 1 to 4 humans, each with Attention, a trait if any, a start room and a d6 routine.
  • 0 to 2 pets, each with a rating, a start room and a d6 routine.
  • The prize: what it is, its size, portions if it’s food, and the Prize Clock — when it’s put down, where, how many steps to get at it, when it moves, and the round it’s gone.
  • Patience.

Portions

A Small food is 1 portion per cat. A Medium food is 1 per cat, plus 1 or 2. A cake or something big is 2 per cat.

Routines that make a house play

  • Give every room at least one result on some routine. A room nobody ever visits becomes a safe place to wait, and waiting is boring.
  • Put the prize’s room on the routine of the human who owns it at least twice.
  • “Stays put” on 1–3 makes a human easy to plan around; on 1–2 they wander more.

Our five houses use: Easy — Patience 4, one human, a soft dog, food, gone on round 9. Standard — Patience 7, two humans and a pet, a thing to carry out, gone on round 9–13 (the bigger the thing, the longer). Hard — Patience 5–6, several watchful humans or a busy cook and a big dog, food, gone on round 7–8.

Test it

Before the night, walk one cat round the map: in through each entrance, to the prize, and out again with it. Check every way you need is big enough for the prize, and that the Den has a door.
