Chapter 4

Rolling the Dice

Most of the time a cat just does things: walks into the hall, sniffs the doormat, sits on the stairs. You only roll when something could go wrong — a climb, a latch, a dog, or anything a human might see.

When to roll

  • When the house says a thing needs a roll: a high counter, a stiff door, a microchip cat flap.
  • When you deal with a pet (Chapter 11).
  • When you haul a Medium or Large prize into another room or out of the house, or eat a portion of the prize (Chapter 8).
  • When you charm a human (that’s always a Sweet roll).
  • When a human can see you and you do anything but walk, sit, wash, stare, or go through an entrance that needs no roll (Chapter 5).

How many dice

Say what your cat does and how — Sneaky, Sweet, Savage or Streetwise — and the Storyteller tells you if it’s plain, Tricky or Hard. Then count your dice:

Counting dice

  1. Start with your approach rating (1–4).
  2. +1 if one of your instincts suits what you’re doing (your Knack; never more than +1).
  3. +1 for each cat helping (at most +2). A helper is in the same room, uses its Action, and doesn’t roll. When cats carry the prize together, the other carriers are helpers.
  4. You can never have more than 5 dice.
  5. Now take dice away: Tricky −1, Hard −2; a pet you’re dealing with takes its rating (the pet’s number, 1–3); and on a Sneaky or Sweet roll, a watching human takes their Attention (only the highest, if several are watching).

If that leaves you with 0 or fewer, you can still try: roll two dice and keep the lower one. It can’t be better than a Scrape (even two 6s). A Push rerolls both dice.

Reading the dice

Roll your dice together and look at the highest one.

Highest dieResultWhat happens
1, 2 or 3FlopIt doesn’t work, and you made a noise.
4 or 5ScrapeIt works, but you made a noise.
6CleanIt works, quietly.
Two or more 6sPurrfectIt works so smoothly that nobody minds: no Mood step, and you gain 1 Cattitude.

Noise brings humans to look (Chapter 6). A Flop means that cat can’t try the same thing again this round (another cat can).

Push your luck

Once per roll, after you see the dice, you can Push: pick up every die that isn’t a 6 and roll them again. Then read the dice again. The cat who rolled pays for it: 1 Cattitude, or 1 step on the Mood meter that roll fills (Chapter 5) — even if nobody saw. A step paid this way goes on with the roll’s own steps, after the reroll, and it always lands — even if the reroll is a Purrfect, and a kid doesn’t double it; a Flop’s fuller meter is the one that was fuller before you pushed. That’s what “at all costs” means: there is always a way to try harder, and it always costs something.

Cattitude

Every cat starts each robbery with 2 Cattitude (3 in a gang of three) and can hold up to 5 (any more is lost). Cats can’t share it, and anything left at the end of the robbery is lost.

Doing it together

When the gang does one thing together — hauling the box into the next room, heaving open a stiff door — one cat leads and rolls; up to two others help (+1 die each, and helping is their Action). The result counts for all of them, and the Mood moves once.

Example

Big Biscuit wants to drive the Okafors’ dog out of the kitchen. That’s Savage (3), and he’s a Scrapper, so +1: 4 dice. The dog’s rating is 2, so 4 − 2 = 2 dice. He rolls 3 and 2 — a Flop. He Pushes for 1 Cattitude and rerolls both: 6 and 1. A 6: Clean. The dog runs to hide behind the sofa.
