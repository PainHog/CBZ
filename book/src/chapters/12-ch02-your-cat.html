Chapter 2

Making Your Cat

Every cat in the gang is a stray with a name the street gave it, a coat, a few scars, and a way of doing things. Making one takes about five minutes. Pick from each table, or roll for it — or roll and keep what makes you laugh.

Seven steps

  1. A street name.
  2. A coat, a mark, and eyes.
  3. Your approaches: 3, 2, 1, 1.
  4. Two instincts.
  5. A body: Lean or Chonky.
  6. A quirk.
  7. Cattitude 2 and 9 Lives.

3. Your approaches

Every cat does things in four ways. Give your cat the ratings 3, 2, 1 and 1, one to each approach, in any order. A rating is how many dice you roll when you do something that way.

ApproachWhen you use itThe gang calls you…
SneakyDoing things without being noticed: hiding, slipping past, creeping, snatching something from under a nose.the Shadow
SweetCharming humans and soft pets: rubbing round legs, purring, the slow blink, begging, being let in and out.the Charmer
SavageForce and speed: hissing, swatting, shoving, bolting, facing down a dog, wriggling free.the Muscle
StreetwiseKnowing how things work: door handles, latches, cupboards, bags, the shortcut over the shed.the Brains

Your 3 is your role in the gang. A gang works best with a bit of everything, but it’s your cat.

To roll instead, roll a d6 for the approach that gets the 3: 1 Sneaky, 2 Sweet, 3 Savage, 4 Streetwise, 5–6 your choice. Roll again for the 2, rerolling an approach you already have. The other two get 1.

4. Two instincts

Roll a d8 twice, rerolling if you get the same one twice, or pick two. Each instinct gives you a Knack — an extra die when what you’re doing suits it — and a temptation, something you find very hard to walk past. Chapter 7 says exactly what each one does.

No d8? Use the same trick as for coats: roll a d6 until it shows 1–4, then roll once more and add 4 on a 4–6.

5. A body

Roll a d6 or pick: 1–3 Lean, 4–6 Chonky.

  • Lean cats fit through Tight gaps: a cracked window, a gap under a gate. They can carry Small things.
  • Chonky cats can’t fit through Tight gaps, but they can carry a Medium thing on their own, and they get +1 die to wriggle free when a human picks them up. There’s a lot of them to hold.

6. A quirk

Roll a d6 or make one up. Quirks are for fun; they never change the rules.

6Talks to pigeons. The pigeons don’t talk back.

7. Cattitude and Lives

Every cat starts each robbery with 2 Cattitude (take two tokens). You can hold up to 5. You earn it by giving in to your instincts and by rolling Purrfects, and you spend it to do better (Chapter 4). Spend it — it doesn’t carry over to the next robbery.

Every cat has 9 Lives. They only matter in a campaign (Chapter 13): when a cat gets adopted, a Life lets it slip out of its new home and back to the gang.

The gang

Give your gang a name. Roll a d6 or pick: 1 the Bin Lid Gang, 2 the Lamppost Lot, 3 the Back Alley Seven (however many you are), 4 the Midnight Moggies, 5 the Puddle Street Paws, 6 the Fish Shop Crew.

Example: making Marmalade Mags

Lena rolls a 4 and a 4: Lucky Mags. She likes “Marmalade Mags” better, so that’s her name. She rolls a ginger tabby coat, a kinked tail and green eyes. She puts her 3 in Sneaky and her 2 in Savage. She rolls a 3 and a 4 on the d8: Box Sitter and Hunter. She rolls a 2 for her body: Lean. Her quirk is a 5: she sleeps on the launderette roof. She takes 2 Cattitude tokens and writes 9 Lives.
