The Birthday Cake

Happy birthday, Mia. Sorry about the cake.

Read this aloud

There are balloons on the gate of Number 5 and music inside. Dad carries a cake in from the car and straight through to the kitchen — chocolate, three layers, under a plastic cover — and the guests are still arriving. In the dining room, a grey parrot shouts “PARTY!” at nobody. Aunt Rosalind, who notices everything, is already sitting in the best chair.

{{SHEET}}

A birthday party: the house is full of people. Dad is in the kitchen with the cake, Aunt Rosalind holds court in the dining room, and Mia and her brother Leo run everywhere. The patio door is open for the guests. At the start of round 5 the cake goes to the dining-room table for the candles, and it will be cut and served at the start of round 8.

How it plays

  • There are four humans and a parrot. You can’t distract them all; work out who will be where.
  • Aunt Rosalind has Attention 2: Sneaky and Sweet are hard under her nose.
  • Two kids double every Cuddle step — and once Cuddle is the fuller meter, any cat’s Flop in front of a kid is 4 Cuddle steps. The Muscle gets scooped here too.
  • The cake wobbles: hauling it is Tricky. Carrying it out whole would be a Clean Getaway, but it takes two shaky hauls past a dining room full of people — eating it is the surer plan.
  • Eating half the cake is a Messy Getaway — and at this house that’s a good night.
