The Okafors’ Big Box

It doesn’t matter what’s in it. It’s a box.

Read this aloud

A van stops outside Number 31. The courier staggers up the path with the biggest box any of you have ever seen. Mrs Okafor signs for it in the doorway while her son Tayo bounces behind her. She drags it into the hall. Somewhere in the kitchen a dog barks once, sleepily, and stops.

{{SHEET}}

The Okafors’ house has a hall, a living room, a long kitchen with a dog door, and a garage with its side door left ajar. Upstairs are the bathroom, the landing and Tayo’s room. Mrs Okafor will unpack the box in the living room, and then she’ll flatten it for the recycling. The gang wants it before that — the whole thing, out of the house.

How it plays

  • This is a carrying job. Get three cats onto the box, then move it room by room. Every room with a human in it is a roll.
  • Waiting it out won’t work for long: at the start of round 5 Mrs Okafor comes for the box, and if it isn’t where she left it, she starts Searching.
  • Tayo is a kid: Sweet rolls he sees fill Cuddle twice as fast. Distract his mum, not him.
  • Noodle sleeps in the kitchen, right on the way out. Don’t wake him — or befriend him; he’s soft.
  • If someone Shoos a cat out through the garage, the side door shuts — and that’s the box’s only way out. Reopen it from inside (Streetwise, Hard).
