The Chicken at Number 12

The best first robbery: one man, one old dog, one chicken.

Read this aloud

It’s a quarter to seven and the street smells of rain. Mr Pell comes up the path with a shopping bag, and out of the top of it pokes a rotisserie chicken, still warm, wrapped in foil. He fumbles for his key. The door opens. From the kitchen comes the thump-thump of his old dog Pudding’s tail. The door begins to close…

{{SHEET}}

Number 12 is a small terraced house: hall, front room, kitchen at the back with a utility room off it, and a downstairs loo. Mr Pell lives alone with Pudding, a soft old spaniel who would rather be fed than fight. He’ll put the chicken on the kitchen counter in its foil and potter about until he eats it.

How it plays

  • The quick way is the kitchen window: it’s a climb, but it puts you next to the chicken.
  • The quiet way is through the cat flap — but it clacks, and then there’s the utility door to open.
  • Mr Pell spends a lot of time in the kitchen. A Charmer who keeps him busy, or a cat who lures him to the front room, is worth a lot. Pudding is easy to befriend.
  • The chicken is gone at the start of round 10. That’s quick: don’t dawdle.

First time running the game?

Run Number 12. Roll Mr Pell’s routine every round, out loud. When the chicken is put down, describe the foil crackling. When the first cat flops in front of Mr Pell, have him say “Oi!” and fill Broom by 2 — the players learn the Mood in one go.
