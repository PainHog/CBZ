The Chicken at Number 12

The best first robbery: one man, one old dog, one chicken.

Read this aloud

It’s a quarter to seven and the street smells of rain. Mr Pell comes up the path with a shopping bag, and out of the top of it pokes a rotisserie chicken, still warm, wrapped in foil. He fumbles for his key. The door opens. From the kitchen comes the thump-thump of his old dog Pudding’s tail. The door begins to close…

{{SHEET}}

Number 12 is a small terraced house: hall, living room, kitchen at the back with a utility room off it, and a downstairs loo. Mr Pell lives alone with Pudding, a soft old spaniel who would rather be fed than fight. He’ll put the chicken on the kitchen counter in its foil and potter about until he eats it.

How it plays

  • The quick way is the kitchen window: it’s a climb, but it puts you next to the chicken.
  • The unseen way is through the cat flap — nobody’s in the utility room, but it clacks, and then there’s the utility door to open.
  • Mr Pell spends a lot of time in the kitchen. A Charmer who keeps him busy, or a cat who lures him to the living room, is worth a lot. Pudding is awake, and easy to befriend.
  • The chicken is gone at the start of round 10. That’s quick: don’t dawdle.
