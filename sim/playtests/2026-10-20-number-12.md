# Playtest: The Chicken at Number 12 (Easy), gang of 5, core rules v0.5, real dice

*2026-10-20 · Verification playtest of `docs/CORE-RULES.md` v0.5, played strictly by its text · House sheet: the `number-12` entry in `sim/houses.mjs` · One agent played the Storyteller and all five players · Every die came from `crypto.randomInt` through a logging script; each one is listed in order in the Dice appendix. `book/REVIEW.md` was read only to understand intent.*

## Summary

- **Outcome: Clean Getaway in round 4.** The prize would have gone at the start of round 6, and Lights Out would have come at the end of round 8. All five cats came in through the cat flap in round 1, and Pearl opened the kitchen–utility door behind them. In round 2 the Clock put the chicken on the kitchen counter:
  - Two-Bin drove off Pudding the dog.
  - Sully Distracted Mr Pell.
  - Joe climbed the counter and Pearl tore the foil, both unseen.

  In round 3 Two-Bin Distracted Mr Pell again (a Flop, Pushed to a Scrape). Pearl, the gang's only Chonky cat, carried the whole chicken into the utility room. In round 4 everyone walked out through the flap.
  - **End state:** Broom 1/4, Cuddle 3/4, Patience 4.
  - Nobody was Shooed, Scooped, Held, shut in or left behind, and no portion was eaten.
- **Cat rolls: 6, plus 1 Push (20 dice):** 4 Clean, 2 Scrape, 1 Flop (Pushed away), 0 Purrfect.
  - The Storyteller and the house rolled 6 dice: 2 routines and 4 temptation picks. Branch B added 1 more.
  - Making the gang took 38 rolls.
  - In all: **52 roll events.**
- **Cat-turns: 20** (5 cats × 4 rounds):
  - 6 had a roll.
  - 4 were spent helping.
  - 2 were a no-roll Action that mattered (Pearl picking up and carrying the chicken out).
  - 3 went on giving in to a temptation. All three were unseen and free, and the Cattitude they earned was never spent.
  - **5 were idle:** the cat only walked in or out.
- **Branch B** tests the other reading of I-01: a human who is in the prize's room when his Distraction ends notices it. Branch B is still a **Clean Getaway, in round 5**, with Broom 3/4 and Cuddle 3/4 and no extra cat rolls. It only works because one unhidden decoy cat pins the Searching Mr Pell in the kitchen (I-23). If a Distracted human Searches blind (I-11), he walks in on the carriers and the next seen cat is Shooed through the flap, which then gets shut. That would be a real fight, and I did not play it.
- **Issues: 23** (0 blockers, 6 major, 17 minor). **Top 5:**
  1. **I-01 (major): a human already in the prize's room never "walks into" it, so he never finds it disturbed.** Mr Pell stood beside the chicken while its foil came off and while it was carried away, and it cost the gang nothing: no Broom, and no Searching. This decided the game, and the simulator reads it the same way.
  2. **I-02 (major): "Moving it past a human is a roll" is silent on the details.** It doesn't say whether a Distracted human counts, whose Action the roll is (the carriers spent theirs picking it up), which approach it uses, or what "past" means. I ruled that a Distracted human doesn't count, which made the carry free.
  3. **I-05 (major): "done first, there" can mean first in the round or first in that cat's turn.** I ruled the cat's own turn. That let every give-in wait until after a Distraction, which made all three temptations free +1 Cattitude.
  4. **I-03 (major): driving off a dog "for 3 rounds" leaves gaps.** The text doesn't say where the dog goes, whether its routine runs, which three rounds count, or what a failed roll does. One Clean roll removed Pudding for the whole robbery.
  5. **I-04 (major): the text doesn't say whether helping costs the helper's Action.** I ruled that it does. With five cats, free help would put +2 dice on every roll.
- **Headline feel:** a good first lesson but too easy with five cats. The gang solved Number 12 in two rounds of real play, with a spare cat every round. One human means one Distraction blinds the whole house, and under I-01 it keeps paying off afterwards. The single tense moment was a good one: a Push paid in Cuddle with a Scoop at stake. The simulator gives five cats here a 96% win rate (`sim/BALANCE.md`, P7).

**Routes for the chicken.** It is Medium, and the rules say "no climbing while carrying", so it can't go out through the climb window (I-18). The front door works only in round 1, and "a cat carrying something can't be Sweet", so it can't be meowed out either. That leaves exactly one way out: **kitchen → (door) → utility → cat flap.**

**Players' plan.** Come in through the free flap and open the utility door in round 1, before the chicken lands. Then, in the round it lands, deal with the dog, Distract Mr Pell, do both steps unseen, and carry it out the way they came.

## Play log

**Standing rulings.** Each one is logged as an issue below, with the other reading.
- Helping uses the helper's Action (I-04).
- A Distracted human doesn't count for "moving it past a human" (I-02).
- A tempted cat gives in at the start of its own turn, and cats act in any order (I-05).
- A driven-off dog runs to a next room with no cats. It skips its routine and ignores cats through the end of the second round after this one (I-03).
- A pet's rating is taken off rolls to deal with that pet (I-06).
- The cat flap makes one noise per cat that goes through it, and that noise is not "on purpose" (I-07).
- Distract needs the cat to be in the human's room (I-10).
- The Clock's "stays in that room this round" beats a noise (I-09).

**Storyteller policy (fair, not mean):**
- Tempt every round that some cat is eligible, picking among eligible cats with a die.
- Follow every routine exactly.
- Break ties with a die.

### Branch B (what-if): Mr Pell notices when his Distraction ends beside the prize

This branch tests the other reading of I-01 (the fix proposed there). It also reads "Distracted until the round ends" as ending *before* the House phase's Searching moves (one side of I-11). Everything up to the end of round 2's cat phase is the same.

- **End of round 2:** Mr Pell's Distraction ends in the kitchen, and he finds the foil off. **Broom +1 → 2/4**, and he starts **Searching**.
- **Round 3:** nothing here depends on Broom or Searching, so the same dice apply: #46/#47 (Joe tempted as a Knocker) and #48/#49 (the Flop, the Cuddle Push, then the Scrape). The players know that a Searching human "step[s] into a next room with one (the carriers first)" if no cat is in sight. So they change one thing: **Joe gives in unseen and stays in the kitchen, unhidden, as a decoy.** Pearl carries the chicken to the utility, and Two-Bin, Sully and Midnight follow.
- **House, round 3:** the two noises do nothing, as in the main line. Then Mr Pell's Distraction ends:
  - The chicken is not where the Clock put it: **Broom +1 → 3/4.**
  - Searching: a cat is in sight (Joe), so he **stays** in the kitchen. Searching humans skip their routine, so no routine roll.
- **Round 4:**
  - **Temptation:** Joe (kitchen), Midnight and Sully (utility) are eligible. **#52: 3 → Midnight.** She gives in unseen (Cattitude → 4).
  - Pearl, Two-Bin, Sully and Midnight go out through the flap, unseen.
  - **Joe walks** from the kitchen to the utility. That's in Mr Pell's view, but walking is free.
  - **House:** the first flap noise draws Mr Pell 1 room into the utility, where he stays.
- **Round 5:**
  - **Temptation:** none (the utility has nothing for Joe's instincts).
  - **Joe walks out** through the flap in view. Going through an entrance with no roll is a Move, and walking is free.
- **Result: Clean Getaway in round 5.** Broom 3/4, Cuddle 3/4, no extra cat rolls.

**Why this matters.** Without the decoy, Mr Pell steps into the utility at the end of round 3 and finds the carriers. In round 4 Broom is at 3/4, so Pearl's roll to move the chicken past him fills Broom on anything but a Sneaky Clean or a Purrfect. Shoo! then makes her drop it, sends her out through the flap, and **shuts it**. The flap is the chicken's only way out.

If a Distracted human Searches "blind" (the other side of I-11), the decoy fails the same way, and Broom reaches 4/4 while nobody is in sight. Two rule gaps (I-01 and I-11) separate a 4-round walkover from a real fight.

## Feel notes

**Is this a good first robbery (Easy)?** Mostly yes as a lesson, but no as a challenge for five cats.
- The good: it teaches the core loop cleanly. Get in, deal with the dog, Distract the human, climb, unwrap, carry it out. Every step was a separate, readable moment.
- The dog stand-off (Two-Bin the Scrapper squaring up to Pudding) was the most fun beat.
- The round-3 Push was the best decision of the game. The gang could pay in Cuddle and risk Two-Bin being Scooped, or let the Distraction fail. An 8-year-old would understand the stakes.
- The bad: the game ended in round 4 with a round to spare before the prize went. The Clock never pressed. Mr Pell is the house's only human, and he saw nothing that mattered, because every important roll happened while he was Distracted, and I-01 means he never notices afterwards either. The simulator puts five cats at 96% here, so a walkover is the typical result, not bad luck.

**Rolls versus idle turns.**
- **6 cat rolls in 20 cat-turns,** and 4 of them were in round 2. Round 1 had one roll and round 4 had none.
- Of the 14 turns without a roll:
  - 4 were helping, which is real teamwork but involves no dice.
  - 2 were Pearl carrying the chicken.
  - 3 were free give-ins. They earned 3 Cattitude that was never spent.
  - 5 were pure walking.
- That is thinner than the simulator's ~8.6 rolls over ~9 rounds for four cats. It still beats the earlier playtests (3 and 0 rolls), so the v0.5 changes (the closed door, the dog that blocks you when you walk in, the Clock holding Mr Pell beside the prize) do add rolls. The robbery is just short.

**Did five cats get in each other's way, or swamp the house?** They swamped it.
- Nothing ever limits cats: any number fit in a room, through the flap, or round the chicken, and extra noise is free because each human answers one noise.
- Round 2 needed four Actions (the dog, the Distraction, the climb, the foil), and five cats had one to spare. With four cats the same plan works. With three it takes two rounds.
- "1 round sooner" didn't bite at all. The only friction was body type: Pearl was the only cat who could carry the Medium chicken alone.
- **Options for five cats at Easy** (for Richard to choose; the simulator should test them):
  - (a) Patience −1 for 5 cats.
  - (b) A second human on the Easy sheet, so one Distraction can't blind the house.
  - (c) Leave it: Easy is meant to be won.

  **I'd recommend (b)**: it makes the Distraction a choice instead of an automatic win, without making new players do more maths.

**Dominant, pointless, confusing.**
- **Dominant: Distract the only human, then do everything unseen.** Two Distractions carried the game. Under I-01 the Distraction pays twice: the human never notices afterwards, so its value lasts past the round.
- **Also dominant: giving in after a Distraction.** It is free Cattitude (I-05).
- **Pointless this game:**
  - Hiding. Being seen at the end of a round has no cost: Mr Pell walked in on all five cats in round 1 and nothing happened, which children will find odd.
  - Cattitude from giving in.
  - The window and the front door (the free flap is better in every way).
  - Eating. Carrying the whole chicken out beats six eating rolls under Mr Pell's eye, so the "portions per cat" rule did nothing.
  - Searching, Shoo!/Scooped!, the Den, wriggling free and Lights Out.
- **Confusing for an 8-year-old:**
  - "A Sneaky Clean 0".
  - "A Flop adds 2 to the fuller meter (its own if level)".
  - Which meter a Push pays into.
  - Counting "driven off for 3 rounds".
  - "Each earlier distraction takes a die".
  - Whether a helper uses up its go.
  - "Moving it past a human".
- **Clear and fun:** the dog, the climb-then-foil steps, and the picture of the one chonky cat walking out of the cat flap with a whole roast chicken.

**Gang creation.** It took 38 rolls for five cats, and 16 of those were just for the 2s. Rerolling 5–6 and the cat's own 3 adds up quickly. A d4, or "shuffle four cards", would be faster. The random gang came out lopsided (no Charmer), and this house relies on Distracting its one human, so a random gang can be badly matched to it. That matters for a first robbery with new players.
