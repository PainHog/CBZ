# Playtest: The Chicken at Number 12 (Easy), gang of 4, rules v0.8, real dice

*2026-10-20 · Final verification playtest of rules v0.8, played strictly by the rulebook source (`book/src/chapters/*.html`: Chapters 4–10 for the rules, Chapter 14 for the house, Chapter 3 for the gang, Chapter 11 for running it). `docs/CORE-RULES.md` was read only as a summary, and where it disagrees with a chapter the chapter won. House data was checked against `sim/houses.mjs` (`number-12`) and the map art's "Ways in" list. The simulator's engine was not read. One agent played the Storyteller and all four players. Every die came from `crypto.randomInt` through a logging script, which logged each roll with its purpose as it was made. Every roll is listed in order in the Dice appendix.*

## Summary

- **Outcome: Clean Getaway in round 4.** The chicken would have gone at the start of round 10, and Lights Out would have come at the end of round 12, so six rounds were left over.
  - **Round 1:** all four cats came in through the cat flap, and Tilly opened the utility–kitchen door.
  - **Round 2:** the Clock put Mr Pell beside the chicken, deaf to noise. Velvet Distracted him, Mags climbed the counter, Tilly peeled the foil and Biscuit picked the chicken up. Mr Pell then found it disturbed (Broom +1) and started Searching.
  - **Round 3:** Velvet Distracted him again, and Biscuit hauled the chicken into the utility with a Purrfect. The Searching Mr Pell followed and spotted Biscuit (Broom +1).
  - **Round 4:** Biscuit hauled the chicken out through the flap in front of Mr Pell (a seen Savage Clean, Broom +1), and everyone walked out.
- **End Mood:** Broom 3/4, Cuddle 2/4, Patience 4. Nobody was Shooed, Scooped, Held, shut in or left behind, and no portion was eaten.
- **Rolls:**
  - **Cats: 7 rolls, 25 dice:** 4 Clean, 2 Scrape, 1 Purrfect, 0 Flop, 0 Pushes.
  - **Storyteller and house: 3 rolls, 3 dice,** all of them Pudding's routine. Mr Pell never rolled a routine: in round 1 he answered noise, in round 2 the Clock held him, and from round 3 he was Searching.
  - **Temptations: 3,** all chosen by the Storyteller, so no dice.
  - **What-if branch: 1 roll.**
  - **In all: 11 dice-log entries.**
- **Cat-turns: 16** (4 cats × 4 rounds). **12 were active:**
  - 7 had a roll.
  - 4 were spent helping.
  - 1 was a no-roll pickup that mattered.

  **4 were idle:** Biscuit, Velvet and Mags only walked in during round 1, and Velvet only walked out in round 4. On top of their turns, the cats made 3 resist decisions (Tilly twice, Velvet once).
- **v0.8's main change worked.** The free noise in round 1 (the flap clacks) pulled Mr Pell into the utility. In round 2, though, the Clock still put him beside the chicken, deaf. When the chicken was touched he noticed at the end of the house's turn: +1 Broom, and he started Searching.
- **The main line hit no decisive ambiguity.** That is a big change from v0.5, where two rule gaps decided the game.
- **Issues: 15** (0 blockers, 1 major, 14 minor). **Top 5:**
  1. **N8-01 (major): does a Distracted human beside the prize notice it when his Distraction ends, or only if he is still there after his routine?** Chapter 6 says "they'll notice", but *Finding it* only checks at the end of the house's turn, after routines. Under the second reading, a gang that waits out the put-down round and then Distracts Mr Pell has a 1-in-3 chance that he wanders off and never notices (Branch W).
  2. **N8-02 (minor): the tracker, the Quick Reference and Chapter 11's checklist say "someone new" beside the prize finds it.** That can be read as "someone who just walked in", which is exactly the v0.5 reading (I-01) that decided that game. Chapter 6 says "every awake human who isn't Searching yet".
  3. **N8-03 (minor): the dog's Charge rule contradicts itself for a cat that climbs in.** One sentence says a cat with no Action left "deals with it first thing next turn". The next says a cat that ends its turn there without dealing with it is "chased back… outside again, if it just came in". That breaks this house's "quick way" in whenever Pudding is in the kitchen.
  4. **N8-04 (minor): Chapter 11 tells the Storyteller to "skip the distraction and lure details in the first robbery".** But Number 12, the first robbery, is built around Distracting its only human: "A Charmer who keeps him busy… is worth a lot". This robbery was won with two Distractions.
  5. **N8-05 (minor): the house's first-time box says the first flop in front of Mr Pell fills "Broom by 2".** The Flop rule puts it on the fuller meter, or its own meter if they're level. The likeliest first roll in front of him is Velvet's Sweet Distraction, which would fill Cuddle.
- **First-robbery verdict: yes, a good first robbery.** It is Easy without being a walkover:
  - Every cat had a job.
  - The order of play answered every question.
  - The last roll was made with Broom at 2/4, where a Flop would have Shooed Biscuit and shut the only exit the chicken fits through.
  - Mean temptations drained three cats' Cattitude.

  Weak spots:
  - The Clock never pressed.
  - Old Pudding, who is in the read-aloud, wandered off on round 1's dice and never mattered.
  - The Storyteller's own advice tells you to skip the one trick this house is built around (N8-04).

**What the house allows.**
- **The chicken's only way out.** It is Medium, and the window is "a climb: Small things only" (map). The front door works only in round 1, and a carrier can't be Sweet, so a meow-out isn't possible. That leaves exactly one route: **kitchen → (shut utility door) → utility → cat flap.**
- **Only Biscuit (Chonky) can carry it alone.**
- **The fewest rolls that can win:** the door, two steps and two hauls, which is 5. No line wins without rolls, because every step and every haul of a Medium prize is a roll, seen or not.

**Players' plan (smart and greedy).**
- **Round 1:** come in through the free flap and open the door before the chicken lands.
- **Round 2:** Mr Pell is deaf and alone, so noise is free. Distract him and do both steps and the pickup under that one Distraction.
- **Afterwards:** haul twice, using a second Distraction or brute Savage.
- **Rejected lines** (see *Other lines* after round 4):
  - Waiting a round: it gains nothing under the reading the book seems to mean (N8-01).
  - Lures: each one leaves a mess worth +1 Broom.
  - Eating the chicken: 5 rolls against 2.
  - Decoys: Searching now goes for the prize first.

## Play log

**Standing rulings** (each one is logged as an issue where the text is unclear):
- The front door works only in round 1, and only for one cat (map, "Ways in").
- In the Clock round, Mr Pell stays in the kitchen for the whole round, house's turn included. He rolls no routine and answers no noise (Ch6 round box; Ch14 "stays there this round").
- Peeling the foil counts as opening, so Tilly's Curious Knack applies (N8-13).
- Four flap clacks in one room are one noise (Ch6: "several noises in one room count as one").
- **Storyteller policy: fair but mean.**
  - Each round, tempt the cat whose Action matters most, or whose giving in would be seen.
  - Never tempt the same cat two rounds running (Ch7).
  - Never tempt when giving in would be free Cattitude.

- All four cats are outside with 2 Cattitude each.
- **Mr Pell** is in the hall with the chicken in his arms (Attention 1).
- **Pudding** is awake in the kitchen (rating 2, soft).
- **Mood:** Broom 0/4 · Cuddle 0/4 · Patience 4.
- **Prize Clock:** put down at the start of round 2. Gone at the start of round 10. Lights Out at the end of round 12.

### Other lines (what-if branches and exploit checks)

**Branch W: wait out the put-down round, then blitz in round 3** (tests N8-01).
- **The idea.** In round 2 the Clock holds Mr Pell beside the chicken, so a Distracted Mr Pell is certain to find it at the end of the round. In round 3 the house's turn rolls his routine *before* *Finding it*.
- **Reading A** (*Finding it* is the only check): a Distracted Mr Pell who rolls 5–6 walks to the living room and never notices. The gang then hauls into the utility unseen, out of his range (three rooms away), and the haul can come out with little or no Mood.
- **Reading B** (Ch6's "When the house's turn comes they look round again — and if the prize has been touched, they'll notice"): he notices as his Distraction ends. He starts Searching, which replaces his routine, so he stays with the chicken. Waiting gains nothing and costs a round of temptation.
- **How I played it.**
  - Round 2: the gang waits in the utility. The Storyteller tempts nobody, because giving in would be free while Mr Pell is deaf and away.
  - Round 3: the gang plays round 2's actions with the same dice (#3–#5).
  - Fresh die for his round-3 routine: **#11: 4 → Kitchen.** He stays beside the chicken, so both readings agree. He finds it (Broom 1/4), starts Searching, and the branch rejoins the main line one round later: a Clean Getaway in round 5.
- **What this shows.** One time in three, Reading A gives a gang that waits a near-free win. That makes waiting through the put-down round the best line, which I don't think the book intends: Ch8 says a Distraction "buys you a round of work — not a round of nobody noticing". **N8-01 is the one ambiguity this house can still turn on.**

**Branch D: Pudding stays in the kitchen** (no dice; she leaves the kitchen on round 1's roll only 1 time in 2).
- **The problem.** Every cat walking in must deal with her before anything else (Ch10 Charge), and Velvet can't Distract Mr Pell until someone has. Round 2 would then need five Actions from four cats. Biscuit drives her off in front of Mr Pell (Savage 3 + Scrapper 1 + 1 Cattitude = 5 − 2 = 3 dice; +1 Broom if it works), and the pickup slips a round.
- **That line ends badly.** By round 4 Broom would be 3 or more with a Searching Mr Pell watching both hauls, so a Shoo! or a third, 2-dice Distraction is likely.
- **The smart answer is to wait.** The cats sit unseen in the utility. Each round Pudding leaves the kitchen on a 3, 5 or 6 (a 6 sniffs her into the utility, where Velvet befriends her unseen with two helpers: 3 dice, and the whole gang is safe from her for the rest of the robbery). The Clock is long enough that waiting costs only temptations.
- **The window is no help.** If Pudding is in the kitchen, a cat that climbs in has no Action left, and N8-03 decides whether it is chased straight back out.

**Exploit checks.**
- **Round-1 lure:** it works, but it is harmless now (see round 1).
- **Decoys:** they don't work. Searching goes for the prize first, so Mr Pell followed the chicken in round 3 even though other cats were in view (old I-23 is fixed).
- **Hiding:** pointless here. The carrier can't hide, and Spotted counts once a round.
- **Sacrifice to the broom:** a deliberate Shoo! in the utility shuts the flap, the chicken's only exit (then Tilly would need Streetwise, Hard to reopen it: 3 + 1 − 2 = 2 dice). In the kitchen it shuts the window, which costs nothing, but Patience drops to 3 for good. This game had no use for it.
- **Eat or carry:** eating means 5 portions, each one always a roll (Biscuit Savage + Foodie = 4 dice). Carrying needs 2 hauls, so carrying is cheaper. A Messy Getaway from half the food is worse than Clean.
- **Lures after round 2:** a lure works on a Searching human ("still answers noise first"), but the mess costs +1 Broom, the same as the find it would replace. No saving.

## Feel notes

**First-robbery verdict: a good first robbery, Easy without being a walkover.**

- **Clear.** The order of play (Clock → tempt → cats → pets → noise → routines → holders → *Finding it*) answered every question in the main line without a house ruling. Every decision had stakes I could state before the roll, as Chapter 11 asks:
  - Should Velvet spend Cattitude on the first Distraction?
  - Should Tilly resist and keep her foil?
  - In round 4: Velvet's third Distraction at 2 dice with a Scoop at risk, or a seen Savage haul with a Shoo! at risk.
- **Fun for an 8-year-old.**
  - The slow blink that blinds Mr Pell.
  - Climb, then crackle the foil.
  - Biscuit's Purrfect: the chicken slides out of the kitchen without a sound.
  - The big brown tabby walking out of the cat flap with a whole roast chicken while Mr Pell, broom-ready at 3/4, watches it go.

  These are great table moments. Two letdowns:
  - **Pudding never appeared.** The read-aloud promises "the thump-thump of his old dog Pudding's tail", but she wandered into the living room on round 1's roll and stayed there. Half of all games will go like this.
  - **Nobody ever gave in to an instinct.** A mean Storyteller only tempts when it hurts, so all three temptations were resisted. Instincts showed up only as a Cattitude tax: 3 of the 5 Cattitude spent. A kids' table will want the Storyteller to tempt for story too, as the GM box says, and a few give-ins are the funniest part of the game.
- **Every cat had something to do.**

  | Cat | Rolls | Other |
  |---|---|---|
  | Tilly | 2 (door, foil) | 1 help, 2 resists |
  | Biscuit | 2 (both hauls) | the pickup |
  | Velvet | 2 (both Distractions) | 1 resist |
  | Mags | 1 (the climb) | 2 helps |

  Only 4 of 16 cat-turns were idle, all of them walking in or out. That is better than the v0.5 five-cat game (5 of 20 idle).
- **Easy, not a walkover.**
  - The gang finished at Broom 3/4.
  - The last roll had a real if small chance of disaster (a Flop on 5 dice, about 1 in 32 before a Push), and the players knew it.
  - Each of three temptations cost a cat its last useful Cattitude.

  But:
  - **The Clock never pressed:** six rounds were left over, and four rounds is the fastest the house can be won at all.
  - **The line is nearly forced.** The chicken has one exit, the house has one human, and the plan is Distract → steps → haul → haul. That's fine for a lesson, but there isn't much to discover. The simulator's "about 9 times in 10" matches how it felt.
- **Maths load.**
  - The cap before subtracting ("5, then take 2 away") came up twice.
  - "Each earlier Distraction costs a die" is the rule most likely to need explaining twice: it is the only one that counts back through the whole robbery.
  - The house's turn has five sub-steps, and with one human most of them are empty: worth saying aloud as "Pudding? No cats. Noise? No. Mr Pell? Searching: he follows the chicken."
- **v0.8's put-down rule in a one-human house.** Mr Pell is deaf in round 2, and nobody else lives there, so all noise that round is free. The gang's two Scrapes in round 2 cost nothing. That's harmless here, because he's standing beside them anyway, but it makes round 2 the obvious work round. The rule that does the real work is *Finding it*.
- **Is waiting better? (N8-01).** If the book means the *Finding it* timing literally, a sharp group should spend round 2 doing nothing and blitz in round 3, hoping for a 5–6 on his routine. I think the book means "they'll notice", and the fix in N8-01 says so.
